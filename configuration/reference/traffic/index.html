traffic¶

Group: Traffic. What moves and what transmits: the fleet, its size, roadside units and the backend.

70 settings. The status column says whether editing a setting changes a run; see how to read these tables. Paths use [] for a list element and * for a map key.

Status Meaning
wired
partial

General¶

Setting Type Unit Default Range or choices Level Status Description
traffic.right_turn_on_red boolean recommended Leave empty to follow the city's rule. basic wired Whether a right turn may be made on a steady red after a full stop. Absent follows the jurisdiction's rule (New York City prohibits it unless signed; most of the US permits it). Whether drivers may turn right on a red light after stopping. Legal in most of the US, banned in New York City.
traffic.dynamic_rerouting boolean advanced wired Whether drivers change route when the travel times they see change or a street closes. On by default.
traffic.reroute_period_s number s [0, 3600]
recommended 0 (off) for a baseline; 60 to 300 to model navigation-app drivers.		 basic wired Congestion-aware rerouting: every vehicle re-plans its route this often, seconds, on the speeds the lanes have shown over the last three minutes, as SUMO's rerouting device does. 0 (the default) routes on free-flow times and re-plans only for a closure. Needs dynamic_rerouting. How often drivers check for a faster way round the traffic, as a navigation app does. Off, every driver keeps the route it chose on free-flowing roads; on, jams push traffic onto parallel streets.
traffic.lane_changes boolean advanced wired Whether drivers change lane to overtake or to reach the lane their next turn needs (MOBIL). On by default; off keeps every vehicle in the lane it started in.
traffic.strategic_lane_choice boolean advanced wired Whether a driver picks the lane their route needs ahead of a turn and changes into it, rather than only routing from the lane they happen to be in. On by default; needs lane_changes.
traffic.keep_junctions_clear boolean advanced wired Whether a driver keeps out of a junction until there is room beyond it and yields to vehicles already inside it ("don't block the box"). On by default.
traffic.gridlock_timeout_s number s [0, 3600]
recommended 300 (SUMO's default); 0 to see raw gridlock.		 basic wired Gridlock resolution: a vehicle that has stood still this long, seconds, is moved on along its route to the next lane with room and counted, as SUMO's time-to-teleport option does (default 300 s). 0 turns it off. When a vehicle has been stuck this long in a jam the network cannot clear, it is moved on along its route and counted, as SUMO does. A run that counts many has unrealistic demand or a network fault.
traffic.critical_gap_factor number [0.5, 2]
recommended 1 (the Highway Capacity Manual's gaps); 0.8 to 1.2.		 basic wired A multiplier on the critical gaps and follow-up times drivers accept at junctions without signals (HCM 6th ed. ch. 20 two-way-stop table: 4.1 s for a major-road left turn to 7.1-7.5 s for a minor-road left). Below 1 drivers take shorter gaps, above 1 they wait for longer ones. Unset is 1, the table. How big a gap in the main-road traffic drivers wait for before pulling out at a junction without lights, or turning right on red where that is allowed: below 1 they squeeze in, above 1 they wait longer.
traffic.permitted_left_gap_s number s [2.5, 8]
recommended 4.5 s (HCM); 4 to 5.5 s.		 advanced wired The critical headway a permitted left turn on a green accepts in the oncoming traffic, seconds. Unset is 4.5 s (HCM 6th ed. ch. 31). The gap in oncoming traffic a driver waits for before turning left across it on a green light.
traffic.turn_lateral_accel_mps2 number m/s² [0, 10]
recommended 5.5 m/s² (SUMO); 3 to 6 m/s².		 advanced wired The average sideways acceleration a turn through a junction is driven at, m/s²: a corner of radius R is taken at sqrt(a·R) at most. Unset is 5.5 m/s² (SUMO netconvert --junctions.limit-turn-speed). How fast drivers take corners through a junction: higher values let them turn faster.
traffic.turn_curve_window_m number m [0, 30]
recommended 0 m (SUMO, average); 6 m to slow for the apex.		 advanced wired The arc length, m, over which a junction connector's radius is judged for its turn speed. Unset (or 0) averages the radius over the whole connector, as SUMO netconvert does; a window (6 m suggested) takes the tightest arc of that length, so drivers slow for the apex of a corner, not only for its average. Whether drivers slow for the sharpest part of a corner (a few metres) or only for its average curve, which is what SUMO does.
traffic.lc_strategic number [0, 10]
recommended Unset (today's MOBIL); 1 (SUMO default); 0.5 to 4.		 advanced wired SUMO LC2013's eagerness for the lane changes a route needs: it scales how far ahead of the turn the change becomes due (10 s at the look-ahead speed, twice that to the left). Setting any lc_* field drives discretionary lane changes by LC2013's motivations instead of MOBIL; unset lc_* fields take SUMO's default 1. How early drivers move into the lane their next turn needs: higher values change lane further before the junction.
traffic.lc_cooperative number [0, 1]
recommended Unset (today's MOBIL); 1 (SUMO default); 0 to 1.		 advanced wired LC2013's willingness to change lane to let a neighbour that must merge in (0 never, 1 always unless it costs speed). See lc_strategic. How willing drivers are to move over to let in a car that must merge into their lane.
traffic.lc_speed_gain number [0, 10]
recommended Unset (today's MOBIL); 1 (SUMO default); 0.5 to 2.		 advanced wired LC2013's eagerness to change lane for speed: the speed-gain threshold is 0.2 / lc_speed_gain (0 turns speed-gain changes off). See lc_strategic. How readily drivers change lane to get past slower traffic; setting any of the lane-change eagerness settings switches to SUMO's LC2013 lane-changing.
traffic.lc_keep_right number [0, 10]
recommended Unset (today's MOBIL); 1 (SUMO default); 0 to 2.		 advanced wired LC2013's eagerness to move back right when the right lane is free (0 off). See lc_strategic. How readily drivers move back into the right lane when it is free, as the keep-right rule asks.
traffic.heavy_vehicle_gap_terms boolean recommended Off (today); on for HCM heavy-vehicle gaps (+1 s, +2 s on wide roads). advanced wired Whether trucks, buses and coaches wait for longer gaps than cars when yielding: the HCM 6th ed. heavy-vehicle terms (Exhibit 20-11: +1.0 s critical gap on a major road of under four lanes, +2.0 s on four or more; +0.9 / 1.0 s follow-up), at unsignalised junctions and on a permitted left. Unset is off. Whether trucks and buses, which accelerate slowly, wait for longer gaps in traffic than cars before pulling out or turning left across it.
traffic.critical_gap_cv number [0, 0.5]
recommended 0 (today); 0.18 for realistic drivers (field spreads 0.15 to 0.2).		 advanced wired The spread of drivers' critical gaps: each driver's is lognormal around the HCM value with this coefficient of variation (0.15-0.2 in field studies, SUMO P1; 0.18 is the realistic preset), at unsignalised junctions, on a permitted left and on a right turn on red. Unset is 0: every driver the HCM value. How much drivers differ in the gap they wait for before pulling out or turning across traffic: 0 gives everyone the same gap.
traffic.time_to_impatience_s number s [0, 3600]
recommended 0 (off, today); 180 s (SUMO) for realistic waiting.		 advanced wired Seconds of standing after which a yielding driver's impatience is full and its critical gap has fallen to impatience_gap_floor of its own (SUMO --time-to-impatience, 180 s). Unset is 0: no impatience. How long a driver waiting to pull out or turn across traffic stands before they accept the shortest gap they ever will; the longer they wait, the smaller the gap they take.
traffic.impatience_gap_floor number [0.3, 1]
recommended 0.7; 0.5 to 0.9.		 advanced wired The critical gap at full impatience as a share of the driver's own. Unset is 0.7. How short a gap a driver who has waited a long time accepts, as a share of the gap they wanted at first.
traffic.impatience_foe_braking boolean recommended Off; on for SUMO-like assertive drivers. advanced wired Whether an impatient driver judges an approaching foe as if it would brake at impatience times 4.5 m/s² (SUMO's blend toward the foe's braking arrival). Unset is off. Whether an impatient driver pulls out counting on oncoming cars to brake for them.
traffic.permissive_sneakers integer vehicles per lane per cycle [0, 4]
recommended 0 (today); 2 (HCM) for realistic left turns.		 advanced wired How many permitted left turners per lane may complete the turn in the amber that ends a permissive green, the HCM's "sneakers" (2 per cycle). Each must cross the stop line before the red. Unset is 0: none. How many drivers waiting to turn left on a green may finish the turn as the light turns yellow, when oncoming traffic stops; without them a left turn into steady oncoming traffic can wait for minutes.
traffic.standing_leader_stop boolean recommended Off (today); on for smooth stops behind queues (the realistic benchmark sets it). advanced wired Whether a driver closing on a standing vehicle brakes at no less than the constant deceleration that stops it at its standstill gap, as at a stop line, instead of being stopped in one step when the car-following model's braking (which scales with its acceleration, low for vans and trucks) arrives too late. Unset is off. Whether drivers coming up behind a stopped car brake smoothly to stop at their usual gap, as they do at a stop line, rather than braking late and stopping abruptly (vans and trucks, whose following model brakes gently, were stopped in one step).
traffic.depart_speed_max boolean recommended Off (today, SUMO's default); on for freeways and other roads entered at speed (SUMO departSpeed=max). advanced wired Whether a trip enters the road already moving, at the highest safe speed there (SUMO's departSpeed="max") instead of at rest (SUMO's default, departSpeed="0"): a freeway entry merges at speed. Unset is off. Whether cars enter the road already moving, at the fastest speed that is safe there, as traffic joining a freeway does, rather than appearing parked in the lane and pulling away from rest.
traffic.lane_bend_speed boolean recommended Off (today); on with turn_curve_window_m 6 for kinked city streets (the realistic benchmark sets it). advanced wired Whether the bends of a through lane's polyline are driven no faster than the turn-speed rule allows (turn_lateral_accel_mps2 over the tightest turn_curve_window_m, 6 m when unset), as a junction connector is. Unset is off. Whether drivers slow for sharp bends in a street's mapped centreline as they do for corners at junctions; without it a car follows a kink in the map at full speed.
traffic.depart_at_speed boolean recommended Off (today); on for realistic map edges (no false halts or stopped-vehicle warnings there). basic wired Whether a vehicle entering the map from outside it (its origin a lane no road of the map feeds, at the map's edge) is put on at the start of that lane at its desired speed, capped by the speed that is safe behind the vehicle ahead and that can still stop at the lane's end, as SUMO's departSpeed="desired" with departPos="base" does. Origins inside the network stay at rest. Unset is off. Whether cars entering the map from outside it are already moving, as real traffic crossing the map's edge is, instead of starting from rest at the edge; cars starting inside the map still start from rest.
traffic.reroute_share number [0, 1]
recommended 1 (every driver, today); about 0.3-0.6 for today's navigation-app use in a US city.		 basic wired The share of drivers who re-route on information, 0-1, drawn once per driver (SUMO device.rerouting.probability). The rest keep the route they chose and detour only round a closure they can see. Unset is 1: every driver. The share of drivers who change route on information (a navigation app or a received V2X warning); the rest keep the route they chose and only detour round a closure they can see.
traffic.reroute_source json (v2xw_mobility::routing::dynamic::RerouteSource) recommended navigation (today); v2x to measure what V2X hazard warnings are worth to routing. basic wired Where those drivers learn that the road has changed: navigation (a navigation app: network-wide, on speeds smoothed over three minutes, and every closure) or v2x (only a DENM or TIM their own unit received and verified, placing a blocking event on their route: local and belief-only). Unset is navigation. Where re-routing drivers learn that the road has changed: a navigation app, which sees the whole network a few minutes late, or only the V2X hazard warnings their own car received, which are local and immediate.

signals¶

Setting Type Unit Default Range or choices Level Status Description
traffic.signals.control string fixed-time, actuated, nema
recommended fixed-time for a repeatable baseline; actuated to study signal timing or SPaT that changes; nema for US controller behaviour (FHWA Signal Timing Manual 2nd ed.).		 basic wired Fixed-time, single-ring vehicle-actuated, or the NEMA dual ring. How the traffic lights decide when to change: on a fixed clock, or actuated by detectors in the road that hold a green while cars keep coming and end it early when the street goes quiet, as most US suburban and many city signals do. nema runs the US eight-phase dual-ring controller: both directions' throughs and lefts time side by side, with pedestrian push buttons, left-turn arrows, coordination and emergency-vehicle priority.
traffic.signals.min_green_s number s [2, 60]
recommended 7 s; 5 to 15 s (FHWA Signal Timing Manual).		 advanced wired The shortest an actuated green runs, seconds. Under actuated control, the shortest a green runs once it starts, even if no more cars arrive, so the queue stored at the stop line can start up and clear.
traffic.signals.max_green_s number s [5, 180]
recommended 45 s; 30 to 60 s (FHWA Signal Timing Manual).		 advanced wired The longest an actuated green runs while another approach waits, seconds. Under actuated control, the longest a green may be held by arriving cars while another approach waits.
traffic.signals.passage_s number s [0.2, 10] advanced wired The gap between detections that ends an actuated green, seconds (the passage time or unit extension).
traffic.signals.detector_m number m [1, 150]
recommended 12.2 m (40 ft stop-line loop, FHWA); 6 to 30 m.		 advanced wired How far back from the stop line the detector sees, metres. Under actuated control, how far back from the stop line the detector loop reaches; cars inside it hold the green.
traffic.signals.detector_placement string stop-line, sumo
recommended stop-line (FHWA) for US practice; sumo to compare with a SUMO actuated run.		 advanced wired Where the extension detectors are: a stop-line presence loop (the default) or SUMO's upstream induction loop at 2 s of travel from the stop line. Under actuated control, where the vehicle detectors sit: a loop at the stop line, or SUMO's single loop per lane about two seconds of travel upstream, which ends the green sooner once a platoon has passed.
traffic.signals.short_link_clearance_m number m [10, 200]
recommended Off for the plans as imported; 30 to 90 m (4 to 12 queued cars at the HCM's 7.62 m each).		 advanced wired Short-link clearance: a driving lane shorter than this between two signalised junctions gets a downstream green that starts just before the upstream green that fills it (crate::actuated::clear_short_links), metres. Absent is off. Where two traffic lights are this close, the downstream light turns green for the short street between them just before the upstream one does, so cars are not held at a green light by a full block ahead (closely spaced signals, FHWA Signal Timing Manual ch. 6).
traffic.signals.green_wave_speed_mps number m/s [1, 40] advanced wired A green wave: every signal's offset set so that a vehicle at this speed meets green after green travelling in green_wave_heading_deg. Absent keeps the plans' own offsets.
traffic.signals.green_wave_heading_deg number ° [0, 360] advanced wired The direction the green wave runs, degrees clockwise from north (90 is eastbound).

traffic.signals.nema.barrier2_phases[] integer NEMA phase advanced partial Barrier2 phases[] Status: The NEMA dual-ring eight-phase controller (control: nema): rings, barriers, lead/lag, the three left-turn modes and both permissive faces, left-turn timing, recalls, yellow, red, pedestrian calls, recall and timing, and coordination run. traffic.signals.nema.lefts string lead-lead, lag-lag, lead-lag
recommended lead-lead, the most common US sequence (FHWA Signal Timing Manual 2nd ed. §5.1.3.6).		 advanced partial Lead-lead, lag-lag or lead-lag lefts. Whether the left-turn arrows come before or after the opposing through traffic's green. Status: The NEMA dual-ring eight-phase controller (control: nema): rings, barriers, lead/lag, the three left-turn modes and both permissive faces, left-turn timing, recalls, yellow, red, pedestrian calls, recall and timing, and coordination run. traffic.signals.nema.permissive_display string fya, green-ball advanced wired The permissive left's face: flashing yellow arrow or circular green.

kerbside¶

Setting Type Unit Default Range or choices Level Status Description
traffic.kerbside.enabled boolean recommended On for city realism; off for a clean baseline. basic wired Whether vehicles stop at the kerb at all. Unset is on. Whether buses stop at stops, taxis pick up and delivery vans double-park, briefly blocking a lane as they do in a real city.
traffic.kerbside.bus_stop_spacing_m number m [100, 2000]
recommended 200 to 400 m for local buses.		 advanced wired The distance from one bus stop to the next along a bus's route, metres. Unset is 260 m. How far apart bus stops are along a bus route.
traffic.kerbside.bus_dwell_mean_s number s [5, 300]
recommended 15 to 60 s (TCQSM).		 advanced wired The mean time a bus stands at a stop, seconds. Unset is 60 s. How long a bus stands at each stop on average.
traffic.kerbside.taxi_share number [0, 1]
recommended 0 outside a downtown; 0.2 to 0.3 in Midtown Manhattan.		 advanced wired The share of passenger cars that are taxis or for-hire cars and stop at the kerb once on their trip. Unset is the jurisdiction's (0, or 0.3 in Midtown Manhattan). The share of cars that are taxis or ride-hail cars making one kerbside stop.
traffic.kerbside.taxi_dwell_median_s number s [5, 600]
recommended 20 to 60 s.		 advanced wired The median time a taxi stands at the kerb, seconds. Unset is 30 s. How long a taxi stands at the kerb for a pick-up or drop-off.
traffic.kerbside.delivery_stop_share number [0, 1]
recommended 0.3 to 0.6.		 advanced wired The share of delivery vans and trucks that make one delivery stop on their trip. Unset is 0.6. The share of vans and trucks that stop once on their trip to deliver.
traffic.kerbside.delivery_dwell_median_s number s [30, 3600]
recommended 5 to 15 minutes.		 advanced wired The median time a delivery stop lasts, seconds. Unset is 600 s. How long a delivery stop lasts.

vehicle_dynamics¶

Setting Type Unit Default Range or choices Level Status Description
traffic.vehicle_dynamics.grade boolean recommended Off (today); on for hilly maps (SUMO's KraussPS slope term). advanced wired The slope term: a vehicle's acceleration less g times the sine of the road's grade, as SUMO's KraussPS has it. Unset is off. Whether hills slow vehicles down: on a climb a driver gets less acceleration from the same pedal, and on a descent the car holds its speed. Needs a map with terrain heights; flat maps are unchanged.
traffic.vehicle_dynamics.power_limit boolean recommended Off (today); on with grade for hilly maps and freight corridors. advanced wired The power term: no more acceleration than the engine's power gives after rolling and air resistance, for the classes with a sourced road-load row (passenger car, tractor-trailer, rigid truck, bus, coach and van). Unset is off. Whether an engine's power limits how fast a vehicle can speed up, so a loaded tractor-trailer crawls up a long climb at about 46 km/h on a 5% grade while a car keeps the limit. Applies to cars, tractor-trailers, rigid trucks, buses, coaches and vans, from cited figures (FTA bus tests, EPA road loads, NCHRP 505); motorcycles have none yet.

emergency_yield¶

Setting Type Unit Default Range or choices Level Status Description
traffic.emergency_yield.enabled boolean recommended Off (today); on for emergency-vehicle studies, where travel time through traffic is the point. advanced wired Whether drivers yield at all. Unset is off. Whether drivers get out of the way of an ambulance, fire engine or police car running its lights and siren: in the US they move to the right-hand lane and stop, clear of the junction, until it has passed (NY VTL 1144(a)). Off, an emergency vehicle has to queue like everyone else.
traffic.emergency_yield.reaction_distance_m number m [1, 500]
recommended 25 m (SUMO's bluelight default); more on a quiet street where a siren carries, less in heavy traffic.		 advanced wired How far behind a driver an emergency vehicle with its siren on is noticed, metres (gap from its front bumper to the driver's rear). Unset is 25 m, SUMO's bluelight device reactiondist default. How far behind a driver an emergency vehicle is when they notice it and start to pull over.
traffic.emergency_yield.red_pass_speed_mps number m/s [0, 10]
recommended 0 (a full stop, the fire-service rule); 2 to 4 m/s for services that let their drivers roll through slowly.		 advanced wired The speed an emergency vehicle slows to at a red before it passes on, once the junction is clear, m/s (UVC §11-106(b)2). Unset is 0: a full stop first, as fire-service practice has it. How slowly an ambulance or fire engine takes a red light it is allowed to pass: 0 means it stops first, then goes once the junction is clear.
