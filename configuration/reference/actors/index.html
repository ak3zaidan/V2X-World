actors¶

Group: Traffic. What moves and what transmits: the fleet, its size, roadside units and the backend.

118 settings. The status column says whether editing a setting changes a run; see how to read these tables. Paths use [] for a list element and * for a map key.

actors.vehicles.demand.fleet_size integer vehicles [1, 50000]
recommended 2 to 50 to watch individual vehicles; 100 to 300 for channel load on this machine.		 basic wired Hold exactly this many vehicles on the road for the whole run: every trip that ends is replaced by a new one, and a trip that cannot enter or has no route is redrawn. The arrival rate is then not used. Absent runs the arrival process. Keeps exactly this many vehicles driving for the whole run: when one finishes its trip a new one sets off, as in a field trial with a fixed test fleet. Leave it empty to let vehicles arrive at the hourly rate instead.

actors.vehicles.drivers.distraction boolean recommended Off (today); on for safety-benefit studies of FCW and EEBL, with realism human. basic wired Drivers look away from the road: on-path dwells and off-path glances drawn from the naturalistic fits of Morando, Victor & Dozza 2019, the vehicle ahead seen as last seen while the eyes are off, and an FCW or EEBL warning from the vehicle's own unit bringing them back (v2xw_mobility::drivers §6). Unset is off. Whether drivers look away from the road now and then, as real drivers do about 800 times an hour (mirrors, instruments, a phone). While they look away they do not see the car ahead brake; a forward-collision or brake-light warning from their own V2X unit makes them look back.

actors.vehicles.gnss.quality string reid-2019 reid-2019, j2945-1, rtk
recommended reid-2019 (the default: city streets are not the open sky J2945/1 tests in); j2945-1 to study a certified device at its test accuracy.		 basic wired The receiver's quality: reid-2019, the measured production receiver (3.07 m horizontal at 68 %, Reid et al. 2019), unless set; j2945-1, a receiver that meets SAE J2945/1's open-sky accuracies (1.5 m horizontal, 3 m vertical at 68 %; heading noise and the low-speed heading latch); rtk, an RTK-corrected one (0.26 m). The urban classes degrade each the same way (J10, 11 §5; 09 §5 keeps the measured receiver the default). How good the vehicles' GPS receivers are: the measured production receiver (about 3 m), one that meets the SAE J2945/1 open-sky accuracies (1.5 m), or RTK (about 0.3 m). Every safety message carries this position.
actors.vehicles.gnss.rtk boolean recommended Off for today's fleet; on to study what precise positioning buys. basic wired An RTK-corrected receiver (Reid 2019's OxTS RT3000 rows: 0.26 m at the 68th percentile) rather than a standard one (about 1.5 m). Unset is standard. Whether vehicles position themselves with survey-grade corrected GPS (about 0.3 m) instead of an ordinary receiver (about 1.5 m). Every safety message carries this position.

recommended 0 (default) to 0.05.		 advanced wired The chance each fix is lost entirely. Unset is 0. The chance each position fix is lost altogether, as in a tunnel.
actors.vehicles.gnss.smoothing_tau_s number s [0, 600]
recommended 100 s (the US real-world presets, RTCA DO-229's smoothing constant); 0 for raw fixes.		 advanced wired The receiver's position-smoothing time constant, seconds: the receiver propagates its solution with its own Doppler velocity and blends each raw fix in at this time constant, as deployed receivers do (gnss/error/gauss-markov module docs). Unset or 0 outputs every raw fix, whose white noise makes consecutive 10 Hz BSMs disagree by metres (R-USFP). How slowly the GPS receiver blends new fixes into the position it reports, steering by its own measured speed in between, as real receivers do. 0 reports every raw fix, which jumps by metres between messages.
actors.vehicles.gnss.static_hold_mps number m/s [0, 1.5]
recommended 0.3 m/s (the US real-world presets); 0 for off.		 advanced wired The receiver's static-hold threshold, m/s: below this measured speed it holds its position and reports zero velocity. Unset or 0 is off; read only with smoothing_tau_s. Below this speed the GPS receiver treats the vehicle as stopped: it holds the position and reports zero speed. Needs the smoothing.
actors.vehicles.gnss.velocity_noise_mps number m/s [0, 0.5]
recommended Unset (default) to compare with earlier runs; 0 with the BSM timing model on, whose fitted output noise then carries the message-level speed and heading noise.		 advanced wired The receiver's velocity output noise, m/s per horizontal axis, drawn afresh at every fix. Unset is the GPS SPS committed velocity accuracy (0.2 m/s at 95 %, σ 0.102 m/s), which as a white 10 Hz noise makes consecutive BSMs' speeds differ by sd 0.14 m/s, where CV-pilot locked devices' differ by 0.045 m/s. 0 leaves the BSM-level speed and heading noise to bsm_timing's fitted output_noise. How much the GPS receiver's reported speed jitters from one fix to the next. Unset uses the GPS service's 95 % accuracy figure, which makes consecutive messages' speeds differ about three times more than real CV-pilot units' do.
actors.vehicles.gnss.stated_sigma_m number m [0, 12.7]
recommended Unset (default) to compare with earlier runs; 0.3 m (Tampa CV pilot) or 2.0 m (Orem) to match a deployment.		 advanced wired The one-sigma horizontal accuracy the receiver states in each BSM's J2735 PositionalAccuracy.semiMajor, metres (the median over devices). Unset or 0 states the model's own honest 95 % radius (about 6.5 m with raw fixes, 5.1 m smoothed). Deployed OBUs state far less than their real error: the CV-pilot medians are 0.30 m Tampa, 0.40 m NYC, 0.50 m Wyoming and a constant 2.00 m Orem (detect-fix3, measured with tools/realdata). The position accuracy each car's radio claims in its messages (one standard deviation). Unset claims the receiver's true error statistics; real deployed units claim much less than their real error, which makes detectors that trust the claim stricter.
actors.vehicles.gnss.stated_sigma_log_sd number [0, 3]
recommended 0.81 with 0.3 m (Tampa's spread); 0 with 2.0 m (Orem's units all claim the same).		 advanced wired The per-device spread of that stated accuracy: a lognormal's log-sd, drawn once per receiver. Unset or 0 states the median on every device; Tampa's per-device medians give 0.81. How much the claimed accuracy differs from one car to the next.
actors.vehicles.bsm_timing.enabled boolean recommended Off (default) to compare with earlier runs; on for honest-trigger studies. advanced wired Turns the model on. Unset or false is off (every existing run unchanged). Gives each car's radio its own message timing, as real units have: some stamp each message with the instant of the GPS fix (locked), others do not (free), and those now and then send a position one fix too old or too new, or the last one again. Real CV-pilot data shows that is what makes honest cars trip the US position-consistency check.
actors.vehicles.bsm_timing.calibration string recommended data/calibration/bsm-timing-cvpilot.yaml when present; unset uses provisional built-in values. advanced wired A v2xw.bsm_timing.v1 calibration file (YAML), as the real-data pipeline writes it (data/calibration/bsm-timing-cvpilot.yaml). Unset is the built-in values, which are PROVISIONAL (CV-pilot sample statistics, research/synthesis/check-realdata-and-vesnos.md §2.2-2.4). A timing calibration file fitted to real CV-pilot messages.
actors.vehicles.bsm_timing.locked_share number [0, 1]
recommended 0.25 (Tampa CV pilot, built in); sweep 0 to 1.		 advanced wired The share of OBUs in the locked class (secMark on the GNSS fix epoch), 0 to 1; the other classes share the rest in proportion. Unset is the calibration's own share (0.25 built in: Tampa's 48 locked of 190 classified IDs). The share of cars whose message time is locked to the GPS fix.
actors.vehicles.bsm_timing.stamp_chain.enabled boolean recommended Off (default) keeps earlier runs; on for the most realistic locked-unit timing (CV-pilot Tampa and Wyoming units). advanced wired Turns the chain on. Unset or false is off. Locked units report the position of the GPS fix itself and stamp the message on the fix or one millisecond early, switching between the two as real units do; when a unit switches to early its position often takes a small lasting step.
actors.vehicles.bsm_timing.stamp_chain.profile string tampa_locked, wyoming_obu, tampa_chain
recommended tampa_chain with data/calibration/bsm-timing-cvpilot-chain-device.yaml for the closest out-of-sample match to Wyoming; tampa_locked is the default.		 advanced wired The rate set: tampa_locked (default; Tampa's locked IDs, a fit set), wyoming_obu (the one Wyoming OBU, P1-E4's held-out source) or tampa_chain (Tampa's chain devices alone, steps out of state 0 only; use it with data/calibration/bsm-timing-cvpilot-chain-device.yaml). Which real units' switching rates to use: Tampa's locked units (stamped early on about 10 % of messages), the Wyoming unit (46 %), or Tampa's switching units alone (tampa_chain, 26 %, whose positions step only while stamped on the fix; use it with the chain-device timing file).
actors.vehicles.bsm_timing.stamp_chain.stay_minus1 number [0, 1]
recommended Unset (the profile's: 0.235 Tampa, 0.566 Wyoming).		 advanced wired P(the next BSM is stamped 1 ms early | this one is); unset is the profile's. The chance that a message stamped early is followed by another early one.
actors.vehicles.bsm_timing.stamp_chain.stay_zero number [0, 1]
recommended Unset (the profile's: 0.918 Tampa, 0.630 Wyoming).		 advanced wired P(the next BSM is stamped on the epoch | this one is); unset is the profile's. The chance that a message stamped on the fix is followed by another on the fix.
actors.vehicles.bsm_timing.stamp_chain.steps boolean recommended On (default) with the chain. advanced wired Lasting position steps at 0 -> -1 transitions, at the profile's rate and size. Unset is true. Whether a switch to early stamping can carry a lasting position step of a few centimetres, as the real units' positioning filter does.
