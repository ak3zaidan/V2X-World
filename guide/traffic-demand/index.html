Traffic demand¶

Demand says when vehicles enter the world, where they start and end, and which way they go. It fills one slot, actors.vehicles.demand:

actors:
  vehicles:
    equipped_fraction: 1.0
    demand:
      kind: mobility/demand/poisson       # which demand model
      rate_veh_per_h: 1500.0              # or rate_veh_per_h_per_lane_km, or fleet_size
      params: {}                          # the model's own parameters
Field Meaning
kind the demand model's id; default mobility/demand/none (nothing arrives)
rate_veh_per_h vehicles per hour offered to the network
rate_veh_per_h_per_lane_km the same as a density, multiplied by the built world's driving-lane length; wins over rate_veh_per_h, and is what makes a scenario fit any city
fleet_size hold exactly this many vehicles on the road: a trip that ends is replaced, and the rate is not used
params the model's parameter struct; unknown keys are refused at load

The vehicle mix, driver behaviour, GNSS and the other vehicle settings are in the actors reference; pedestrians and cyclists are actors.vru.

The demand kinds¶

kind Arrivals params
mobility/demand/poisson (alias …/poisson-thinned) a thinned Poisson process with a time-of-day shape the Poisson struct below, plus an optional od object
mobility/demand/tr36885-drop one placement of vehicles along every lane, spaced by 3GPP TR 36.885 Table A.1.2-1 or TR 37.885 §6.1.2 variant, mean_gap_time, speeds, …
mobility/demand/sumo-rou a SUMO .rou.xml: vTypes, routes, flows {path, probability_flows: bernoulli \| equal-spacing}
mobility/demand/routes a v2xw/routes/1 file (an assignment's or a count sampler's output) {path}
mobility/demand/trips a v2xw/trips/1 file, optionally assigned at load {path, assign?}
mobility/demand/od-matrix an OD matrix turned into trips {matrix \| cells \| gravity, zones, od2trips?, assign?}
mobility/demand/counts routes and trips that reproduce observed link counts {counts, sampler?, candidates?}
mobility/demand/gtfs timetabled buses and street-level rail the transit struct below
mobility/demand/purposes a day of home-based tours by trip purpose {daily_trips, purposes?, run_start_s_of_day?, seed?}
mobility/demand/composite several of the above at once {parts: [{kind, params, rate_veh_per_h?}], utc_offset_s?}
mobility/demand/none nothing

Paths are read relative to the working directory, as world.source.path is. Times of day are local: utc_offset_s (for example -28800 for Pacific standard time) turns the scenario's UTC time.t0 into the local start time that timetables, matrices and profiles are written in.

Poisson arrivals¶

mobility/demand/poisson is an exact simulation of an inhomogeneous Poisson process by thinning: candidates arrive at a boosted constant rate and each is kept with the probability the time-of-day shape gives.

\[ \Delta t \sim \mathrm{Exp}(\lambda\, b), \qquad P(\text{keep at } t) = \frac{m(f)}{b}, \qquad f = \frac{t}{T} \]

with \(\lambda\) the base rate, \(b \ge \sup m\) the candidate boost (candidate_boost) and \(T\) the run length. Shapes (profile):

profile \(m(f)\)
uniform (default) \(1\)
rush \(0.2 + 0.8\,\min\!\left(1,\ e^{-((f-0.25)/0.09)^2} + e^{-((f-0.75)/0.09)^2}\right)\): two peaks, a quiet midday
night \(0.15 + 0.25 f\)

These shapes follow the fraction of the run, whatever its length. A run of hours or days uses daily instead: a factor for each hour of the local civil day, interpolated between hour centres, with weekday and weekend curves. The shipped curve (nyc-dot-manhattan, or -weekday/-weekend on every day) is NYC DOT's Automated Traffic Volume Counts for Manhattan, 2015 to 2026, normalised so the weekday factors average 1 (night lull 0.26 at 03:00, morning peak 1.30 at 08:00, evening plateau 1.33). rate_veh_per_h is then the mean weekday rate. The curve is a borough average, not the counts at the streets of a world, and the clock is standard time from a fixed UTC offset.

scenarios/manhattan-day.yaml (excerpt)
demand:
  kind: mobility/demand/poisson
  rate_veh_per_h: 300.0
  params:
    daily: {curve: nyc-dot-manhattan}     # utc_offset_h defaults from the world's longitude

Other Poisson parameters: max_total_vehicles (a cap, 0 for none), fleet (the class mix), speed (how a trip's desired speed is drawn; default SUMO-style class speed deviation), fleet_size, and od (where trips start and end: uniform or gravity destinations with w(h) = e^{−h/scale} over junction hops, optional perimeter origins). Every parameter with its source: mobility/demand/poisson-thinned.

The Studio's named rates for Midtown (crates/v2xw-engine/src/scenario/options.rs): Night 500, Light 1,500, Normal (midday) 3,000, Rush hour 4,500, Gridlock 9,000 veh/h. Their ratios are cited (HCM 6th ed. design-hour factor about 9 % of daily traffic; FHWA Traffic Monitoring Guide 2016, about 6 % at midday and 1 % in the small hours); their level is scaled to what the Midtown extract runs interactively.

Demand can also change during a run through the timeline: {t: 60.0, until: 240.0, type: demand.multiplier, value: 1.3} (events).

OD matrices¶

demand:
  kind: mobility/demand/od-matrix
  params:
    zones: {kind: grid, cols: 2, rows: 2}
    cells:
      - {from: r0c0, to: r1c1, count: 120}
      - {from: r1c1, to: r0c0, count: 120}
    assign: {max_iterations: 4}
  • Formats (matrix: path): PTV VISUM/VISSIM O-format ($O, one from to count line per cell) and V-format ($V, a full square matrix), the two SUMO's od2trips reads, and a plain CSV from,to,count[,begin_s,end_s]. Or cells inline.
  • Synthesis (gravity): a doubly-constrained gravity model \(T_{ij} = a_i b_j P_i A_j f(c_{ij})\) balanced by Furness's method (Furness 1965; Ortúzar and Willumsen, Modelling Transport 4th ed., ch. 5), with exponential, power or combined deterrence. β is uncalibrated; the card gives Hyman's method (1969) to fit it.
  • Zones: a grid of the world (grid:CxR on the command line), weighted by building floor area.
  • Matrix to trips: each cell's count is scaled and rounded stochastically; departures are uniform in the interval or follow a departure profile; origin and destination lanes are drawn by zone weights. Draws are keyed by the cell, so changing one cell changes no other cell's trips.

Link counts¶

A count is vehicles past a place in an interval. The place is an edge, a lane, or a real station's coordinate (with direction), snapped to the nearest road of matching heading:

id,kind,ref,begin_s,end_s,count
5th-ave-n,point,45.5190 -122.6765 0,0,3600,612
e17,edge,17,0,900,140

mobility/demand/counts builds routes and trips that reproduce them (max-entropy or greedy sampling). Any routed demand is checked against counts with the GEH statistic and the acceptance criterion of UK DfT TAG Unit M3.1 (2020) Table 2: GEH < 5 on at least 85 % of counts.

Route choice and dynamic traffic assignment¶

assign (on od-matrix and trips, and the v2xw-demand assign command) runs an iterative dynamic traffic assignment in the manner of SUMO's duaIterate (Gawron 1998):

  1. every trip takes its free-flow time-dependent shortest path, or a saved assignment's;
  2. a network loader drives the routes and measures lane travel times per interval;
  3. the relative gap \(\sum (c_\text{used} - c_\text{best}) / \sum c_\text{used}\) is measured (Chiu et al., Dynamic Traffic Assignment: A Primer, TRB E-C153, 2011, §4);
  4. stop when the gap is below gap_tolerance (after min_iterations) or at max_iterations;
  5. otherwise the route-choice model updates each trip's probabilities, each trip draws its next route, and the loop repeats.
Route choice (assign.choice.model) Source
gawron (default) Gawron 1998, pairwise update with smoothed costs; duarouter's default
logit multinomial logit (Ben-Akiva and Lerman 1985) with the C-logit commonality correction (Cascetta et al. 1996)
msa method of successive averages, 1/k onto the best route at iteration k (Sheffi 1985, ch. 12)
Network loader (assign.loader.model) What it is
queue (default) event-driven point queues with signal gating (Vickrey 1969), no spill-back
bpr static link performance \(t = t_0 (1 + \alpha (v/c)^\beta)\) (Bureau of Public Roads 1964)
micro the engine's own microscopic mobility driving the routes; most faithful, slowest

The result is a v2xw/routes/1 file: every trip with its alternatives, each route's cost and probability, the chosen one, the world hash it was made for, the parameters and the iteration log. mobility/demand/routes runs it. During a run, mobility/routing/dynamic-reroute re-plans at the next junction on a closure.

Trip purposes¶

mobility/demand/purposes generates a day as tours: a home-based tour leaves home at a time drawn from its purpose's departure profile, stays for a drawn duration and returns; non-home-based and commercial trips are single legs (purposes after NCHRP Report 716, ch. 4). Activities are drawn with distance decay \(w_j e^{-d_{ij}/d_0}\). The shipped purpose shares, profiles and durations are uncalibrated shapes, marked todo-calibrate with the plan of fitting the 2017 NHTS; a study passes its own per-purpose specification in purposes. Days are generated one at a time, keyed by (seed, day, purpose, tour), so a week costs one day's memory and a resumed run regenerates the same day.

Public transport (GTFS)¶

scenarios/portland-transit.yaml (excerpt)
demand:
  kind: mobility/demand/composite
  params:
    utc_offset_s: -25200                  # 15:00 UTC is 08:00 Pacific daylight time
    parts:
      - kind: mobility/demand/poisson
        rate_veh_per_h: 1000.0
      - kind: mobility/demand/gtfs
        params:
          feed: worlds/cache/portland.gtfs
          date: 20261007
          dwell: {model: scheduled, min_s: 4.0}
          hold_early: true

The GTFS kind places each stop on the network, runs buses, trolleybuses and street-level rail (route types 0, 3, 5, 11, 700–799, 900–999 unless route_types says otherwise), with a dwell model and optional holding of early vehicles. Fetch and clip a feed, then check what the stop matcher made of it before running:

worlds/fetch.sh portland
worlds/fetch-gtfs.sh portland 20261007            # worlds/cache/portland.gtfs/
v2xw-demand gtfs --scenario scenarios/portland-transit.yaml \
    --feed worlds/cache/portland.gtfs --date 20261007 --start 08:00

The v2xw-demand tool¶

Precomputes demand outside a run, against exactly the world a scenario builds, so the files' world hash matches when the scenario loads them. Every command and flag: Command-line tool.

v2xw-demand od2trips --scenario S.yaml --matrix city.od --zones grid:4x4 --start 07:00 --out trips.json
v2xw-demand assign   --scenario S.yaml --trips trips.json --choice logit --loader queue --out routes.json
v2xw-demand check    --scenario S.yaml --counts counts.csv --routes routes.json

assign prints one line per iteration (relative gap, mean travel time, arrivals, share switched, mean alternatives) and whether it converged. check prints observed and modelled counts with GEH per row and the TAG M3.1 verdict.

Adding a demand model¶

Implement v2xw_mobility::traits::Demand, add its id to v2xw_demand::scenario::KINDS and an arm to build; the engine forwards every kind in KINDS to that crate, so nothing in the engine changes. Each family (route choice, loader, dwell, deterrence, departure profile) has its own trait and spec enum, and each module ends with a "How to add another" note. See Extending: models.
