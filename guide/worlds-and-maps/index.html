Worlds and maps¶

A world is everything that does not move: lanes, junctions with their connectors and conflict matrices, signal plans, crossings, buildings, terrain, vegetation, roadside sites, and a provenance record. v2xw-world builds it once per run, quantises it, hashes it (world_hash in the manifest) and every other crate reads it. A scenario gets its world one of three ways:

Way Scenario says Needs
a city of the library city: <id>, or --city <id> the extract in worlds/cache/<id>.osm.xml
an explicit source world.source: {kind: …} the file it names, or nothing for a procedural generator
a city replacing the file's world v2xw run <scenario> --city <id> as the first row

Every world setting: world reference. The import models and their equations: Traffic and mobility.

The city library¶

worlds/cities.yaml (schema v2xw/cities/1, compiled into the binary so every interface lists the same cities) holds the largest city of every US state and of DC, the most-studied connected-vehicle sites (the New York and Tampa USDOT pilots, Ann Arbor, Columbus, Pittsburgh, the large Texas and California cores) and Berlin-Mitte for the European stack. Each entry is a downtown box of about 1.5 km × 1.5 km:

Field Meaning
id the city's name everywhere; its extract is worlds/cache/<id>.osm.xml
bbox [south, west, north, east], WGS 84; its south-west corner is the world origin, so re-fetching cannot move a coordinate
highway_preset the jurisdiction's default speed limits and rules of the road (crates/v2xw-world/src/jurisdiction.rs cites each)
utc_offset_h standard-time offset
fetched the download date, which becomes world.imported_at
drive_lane_km, signals driving-lane length and signalised intersections, measured by city_audit; used to rescale demand 
v2xw cities                       # every city by state, [not fetched] where the extract is missing
v2xw cities --json                # the registry as JSON
worlds/fetch.sh boston            # one extract
worlds/fetch.sh --all             # every missing one (one at a time, FETCH_PAUSE_S apart)
worlds/fetch.sh --list            # ids and whether each extract is present
worlds/fetch.sh mytown 45.51,-122.69,45.53,-122.66   # any box: S,W,N,E

fetch.sh asks the Overpass API for every node and way in the box, the turn restrictions and the building multipolygons, leaves out far-reaching relations, retries with backoff, falls back to mirrors (OVERPASS_URL puts yours first) and rejects a truncated answer. V2XW_WORLDS_DIR points the engine at another extract directory.

What --city changes¶

A scenario says what happens; a city says where (crates/v2xw-engine/src/city.rs). To make any scenario fit any city:

  1. The world. The city's extract, box, highway preset and fetch date go into world; the scenario's building and signal options stay.
  2. What belonged to the old city. Roadside units at explicit positions become an actors.rsu_placement of the same count and roles; a fixed arrival rate becomes a rate per kilometre of driving lane; the clock keeps its local time of day; a closure of a named street becomes target: auto; a focus box is dropped.
  3. The built world. Once imported, a per-lane-km rate becomes vehicles per hour and placed roadside units stand at the busiest signalised intersections (most approach lanes first, at least min_spacing_m apart).

So a scenario written with rate_veh_per_h_per_lane_km, rsu_placement, target: auto and at: busiest-junction runs on every city unchanged. Before a city is listed it is imported and run under the traffic auditor; a city that fails is listed under dropped: with the reason.

World sources¶

world.source.kind selects the importer:

kind Reads Importer
osm-xml path to a .osm/.osm.xml extract, optional bbox (min_lat_deg, min_lon_deg, max_lat_deg, max_lon_deg) world/source/osm
sumo-net path to a SUMO net.xml world/source/sumo-net
open-drive path to an ASAM OpenDRIVE .xodr, converted through netconvert world/source/opendrive
procedural generator and params world/source/procedural-grid, -radial, -random

osm-bbox (fetch at load) and legacy-json are accepted by the type but have no importer in this build; a scenario naming them is refused with UnsupportedSource.

world:
  source:
    kind: osm-xml
    path: worlds/cache/manhattan.osm.xml
    bbox: {min_lat_deg: 40.744, min_lon_deg: -73.990, max_lat_deg: 40.762, max_lon_deg: -73.968}
  highway_preset: urban-us-nyc
  imported_at: "2026-09-18T00:00:00Z"

world:
  source: {kind: sumo-net, path: worlds/sumo/grid3.net.xml}
  imported_at: "2026-10-07T00:00:00Z"

world:
  source:
    kind: procedural
    generator: world/source/procedural-grid
    params: {cols: 13, rows: 34, block_x_m: 274.0, block_y_m: 80.0,
             lanes_per_direction: 2, signalised: true, cycle_s: 60.0,
             crossings: true, block_buildings: true}
  imported_at: "2026-09-18T00:00:00Z"

OpenStreetMap¶

The importer builds drivable, pedestrian and cycle networks, junctions with connectors and conflict matrices, synthesised fixed-time signals, buildings with sourced heights, land-use zones and a provenance record. It needs a speed preset for roads with no maxspeed tag, and has no default: a fallback speed limit is a statement about a jurisdiction. Presets: urban-us-nyc, urban-us-portland, urban-de, sumo-german (netconvert's table, for comparability with SUMO), and an urban-us-<state> preset for every state (world.highway_preset).

OpenStreetMap carries no signal timing, so plans are synthesised; the world.signals block sets the cycle (default 90 s), all-red, pedestrian walk (7 s, MUTCD 2009 §4E.06) and walking speed (1.067 m/s), green-wave coordination and the joining of close junctions (netconvert's --tls.join). These apply to imported worlds only. world.service_roads keeps or drops highway=service alleys and driveways.

v2xw import-osm runs the same importer outside a run and writes the three world formats:

v2xw import-osm worlds/cache/portland.osm.xml out/portland --speed-preset urban-us-portland
File Format
world.vwb the browser's binary vwp-world/1 payload (protocol §4)
world.json the same payload as JSON
world.v2xw the engine's native binary, an exact round trip (same content hash)
report.txt what the importer kept, dropped and inferred, with every anomaly counted

The world validation report (geometry a car or pedestrian cannot use, every imported attribute against its source tags) runs as cargo run -p v2xw-world --example world_report -- <extract> --preset <p> [--baseline worlds/validation/<city>.json]; with --baseline it fails when a check's count rises above the committed ceiling.

SUMO net.xml and OpenDRIVE¶

sumo-net reads edges, lanes, junctions, connections, internal connectors, right-of-way matrices, traffic-light programmes and crossings into the same lane-level world, counting an anomaly wherever the formats disagree. Each SUMO edge id is kept as the edge's name, so a SUMO lane id is always a world lane, which is what the TraCI co-simulation relies on (SUMO co-simulation). world.sumo.signal_programmes runs actuated, delay-based and NEMA tlLogic as live controllers (as-file, the default) or flattens them (static). OpenDRIVE goes through netconvert, then the same reader.

Procedural networks¶

Generator Shape Main parameters (defaults)
world/source/procedural-grid a rectangular lattice of two-way streets; the reference world for tests and the TR 36.885 urban grid cols 6, rows 6, block_x_m/block_y_m 120, lanes_per_direction 1, signalised false, cycle_s 60, amber_s 3, ew_green_share 0.5, crossings, block_buildings, rsu_at_junctions, corner_radius_m
world/source/procedural-radial spokes from a central plaza crossed by ring roads arms 6, rings 6, ring_spacing_m 120, dropout 0, signalised
world/source/procedural-random an irregular network grown by random attachment, reproducible from its seed iterations 150, min_distance_m 80, max_distance_m 250, seed 1, min_angle_deg 45

Every parameter with its source: model parameters. The random generator is the only one that draws numbers, from one declared stream.

Buildings, terrain and vegetation¶

Layer Settings Notes
Buildings world.buildings.enabled, keep_holes, metres_per_level footprints from OSM with heights from tags; metres_per_level (the building:levels rule) is marked todo-calibrate. Buildings obstruct radio links
Terrain world.terrain.dem, world.terrain.drape a DEM raster resampled bilinearly onto the world grid, voids filled from the nearest sample and counted. drape: true lifts every lane point, junction, building, site and signal head onto it (and changes the world hash)
Vegetation world.vegetation keeps OSM woods, tree rows and single trees as a layer; radio.channel_model.foliage charges the ITU-R P.833 excess loss through it

DEM formats (crates/v2xw-world/src/dem.rs): SRTM .hgt (NASA SRTMGL1 one-degree tiles, corner read from the file name) and ESRI/USGS ASCII grid (.asc), the path for Copernicus GLO-30 after gdal_translate -of AAIGrid. Without a DEM every height is 0 and existing world hashes are unchanged. scenarios/real-world/portland-hills.yaml is the worked example, on worlds/dem/portland-downtown.asc (provenance in worlds/dem/portland-downtown.md).

Provenance and the cache¶

Every world carries a provenance record: the source kind and id, the source box, the import date (from the scenario, never a clock, and excluded from the world hash), tool versions, projection and origin, the transformations applied, each layer's licence and counts of what was dropped. Worlds built from OpenStreetMap carry the ODbL attribution.

world.cache names a directory: the world is read from <key>.v2xwworld there when present, imported and written there when not. The key hashes the world section and the source files' contents, so an edited extract is a different world. The cached form round-trips exactly, so a cached run is the same run; a corrupt entry is re-imported, never an error.
