# Anomaly Detection in the Internet of Vehicular Networks Using Explainable Neural Networks (xNN)
Saddam Aziz, Muhammad Talib Faiz, Muideen Adegoke, K. H. Loo, Kazi N. Hasan, Linli Xu, Muhammad Irshad · Mathematics 2022 · https://doi.org/10.3390/math10081267 · relevance: medium · basis: abstract

**Problem:** It is increasingly difficult to identify complex cyberattacks in a wide range of industries, such as the Internet of Vehicles (IoV). The IoV is a network of vehicles that consists of sensors, actuators, network layers, and communication systems between vehicles.
**Approach:** Communication plays an important role as an essential part of the IoV. Vehicles in a network share and deliver information based on several protocols.
**Tools and scale:** After extracting the best features for anomaly detection, we applied a novel model, i.e., an Explainable Neural Network (xNN), to classify attacks in the CICIDS2019 dataset and UNSW-NB15 dataset separately.
**Results:** In dataset 1 (UNSW-NB15), xNN performed well, with the highest accuracy of 99.7%, while CNN scored 87%, LSTM scored 90%, and the Deep Neural Network (DNN) scored 92%. xNN achieved the highest accuracy of 99.3% while classifying attacks in the second dataset (CICIDS2019); the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) achieved 87%, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) achieved 89%, and the DNN achieved 82%. The suggested solution outperformed the existing systems in terms of the detection and classification accuracy.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- Many machine-learning and deep-learning (DL) models have been implemented to detect malicious attacks; however, feature selection remains a core issue.
**Overlap with our engine:** partial — V2X/VANET attack or intrusion detection; overlaps our misbehaviour-detection component only at the detector level, without SCMS reporting, revocation or closed-loop safety measurement.
**Novelty implications:** Does not rule out any candidate direction; it does not model SCMS certificates, linkage-based revocation, per-message joint ground truth or counterfactual reruns.
