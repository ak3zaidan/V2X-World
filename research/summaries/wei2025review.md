# A Review on Anomaly Detection for Connected Vehicles Using Deep Reinforcement Learning
Matthias Weiß, Friedrich Sautter, Maurice Artelt, Philipp Grimmeisen, Nasser Jazdi, Michael Weyrich · 2025 IEEE 30th International Conference on Emerging Technologies and Factory Automation (ETFA) 2025 · https://doi.org/10.1109/etfa65518.2025.11205543 · relevance: medium · basis: abstract

**Problem:** The transition to connected vehicles (CVs) is shaping the future of autonomous and highly connected vehicles.
**Approach:** As the vehicles’ reliance on increasingly complex software increases, more central points of failure are introduced. This necessitates more thorough and scalable anomaly and intrusion detection systems.
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the abstract.
**Results:** Reinforcement Learning especially has shown the potential to increase configurability and provide end-to-end solutions. The results of the survey indicate that many of the identified RL formulations are not inherently superior from supervised learning approaches.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- The transition to connected vehicles (CVs) is shaping the future of autonomous and highly connected vehicles.
- The problem formulations for different approaches are compared, their advantages and challenges are discussed.
- However, RL techniques can result in better configurability of anomaly detection system, enabling site reliability engineers to efficiently adjust the model via reward functions.
**Overlap with our engine:** partial — same area (V2X/vehicular security or networking) with a narrower, mechanism-level focus.
**Novelty implications:** Does not remove any of our claims beyond its specific mechanism; we cannot claim to be first on that mechanism. The joint closed-loop traffic/radio/PKI evaluation with counterfactual ground truth remains open as far as this abstract shows.
