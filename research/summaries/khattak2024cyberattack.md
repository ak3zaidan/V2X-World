# Cyberattack Monitoring Architectures for Resilient Operation of Connected and Automated Vehicles
Zulqarnain H. Khattak, Brian Lee Smith, Michael D. Fontaine · IEEE Open Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems 2024 · https://doi.org/10.1109/ojits.2024.3391830 · relevance: medium · basis: abstract

**Problem:** The deployment of connected and automated vehicles (CAVs) may enhance operations and safety with little human feedback.
**Approach:** Automation requires the use of communication and smart devices, thus introducing potential access points for adversaries. This paper develops a prototype real-time monitoring system for a vehicle to infrastructure (V2I) based CAV system that generates cyberattack data for CAV operations under realistic traffic conditions.
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the abstract.
**Results:** The LSTM showed the highest accuracy of 98% and outperformed the other algorithms. The results reveal that a fake basic safety message (BSM) attack on even a single CAV causes the traffic stream to become significantly unstable and increase safety risk without the monitoring system.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- The monitoring system, however, reverts the system to a safe state of operation and reduces the negative impacts of cyberattacks.
- The findings have implications for the design of future resilient systems.
**Overlap with our engine:** partial — same area (V2X/vehicular security or networking) with a narrower, mechanism-level focus.
