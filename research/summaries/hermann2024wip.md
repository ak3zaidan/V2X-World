# WIP: A Trust Assessment Method for In-Vehicular Networks using Vehicle Risk Assessment
Artur Hermann, Nataša Trkulja, Anderson Ramon Ferraz de Lucena, Alexander Kiening, Ana Petrovska, Frank Kargl · Proceedings Symposium on Vehicle Security &amp; Privacy 2024 · https://doi.org/10.14722/vehiclesec.2024.23016 · relevance: medium · basis: abstract

**Problem:** —Future vehicles will run safety-critical applications that rely on data from entities within and outside the vehicle.
**Approach:** Malicious manipulation of this data can lead to safety incidents. In our work, we propose a Trust Assessment Framework (TAF) that allows a component in a vehicle to assess whether it can trust the provided data.
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the abstract.
**Results:** The feasibility of the proposed concept is showcased using an in-vehicle application with two different scenarios. Based on the initial results presented in this paper, we see an indication that a trust assessment based on quantifying security controls represents a reasonable approach to provide trust opinions for a TAF.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- —Future vehicles will run safety-critical applications that rely on data from entities within and outside the vehicle.
- One particular challenge in this approach is the appropriate quantification of trust.
**Overlap with our engine:** partial — same area (V2X/vehicular security or networking) with a narrower, mechanism-level focus.
**Novelty implications:** Does not remove any of our claims beyond its specific mechanism; we cannot claim to be first on that mechanism. The joint closed-loop traffic/radio/PKI evaluation with counterfactual ground truth remains open as far as this abstract shows.
