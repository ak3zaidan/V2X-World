# Federated learning-based lightweight digital certificate authentication and anomaly detection framework for IoT environments
Wenjun Li, Wenlong Han, Muheng Sui et al. · Scientific Reports 2026 · https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-67014-x · relevance: low · basis: abstract

**Problem:** The exponential growth of Internet of Things (IoT) deployments has strained conventional security architectures, exposing two interlinked weaknesses: certificate handling designed for servers overwhelms microcontroller-class hardware, and isolated anomaly detection leaks privacy once behavioural data leaves the device.
**Approach:** The exponential growth of Internet of Things (IoT) deployments has strained conventional security architectures, exposing two interlinked weaknesses: certificate handling designed for servers overwhelms microcontroller-class hardware, and isolated anomaly detection leaks privacy once behavioural data leaves the device.
**Tools and scale:** Evaluation on the N-BaIoT dataset, roughly 7 million records from nine commercial devices under Mirai and BASHLITE traffic, yields 95.2% detection accuracy, 1.6 percentage points below the centralized upper bound.
**Results:** The certificate occupies 88 bytes, a 92.7% reduction relative to a typical X.509 credential, and a complete authentication exchange, comprising public-key reconstruction, a verifier challenge, proof of private-key possession over the bound transcript and session-key confirmation, completes in 12.3 ms on an ESP32, roughly five times faster than an RSA-2048 baseline measured on the same board.
