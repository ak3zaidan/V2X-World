# Can cyberattacks on adaptive cruise control vehicles be effectively detected?
Tianyi Li, Shian Wang, Mingfeng Shang, Raphael Stern · 2024 IEEE Intelligent Vehicles Symposium (IV) 2024 · https://doi.org/10.1109/iv55156.2024.10588379 · relevance: high · basis: abstract

**Problem:** Automated Vehicles (AVs), particularly those with Adaptive Cruise Control (ACC), are increasingly integral to intelligent transportation systems, but they bring new cybersecurity challenges.
**Approach:** This study explores the subtleties of cyberattacks targeting ACC vehicles, specifically through false data injection, and assesses their impact on traffic dynamics. We innovatively adapt and implement strategically designed cyberattacks in simulations, providing a realistic evaluation of their effects on traffic.
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the available text unless given above (topic: platoon/CACC attacks).
**Results:** The open-sourced experiment code is made available at https://github.com/tianyi17/simulations_IV24.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- This research accentuates the urgent need for more sophisticated detection and defense strategies to protect ACC vehicles against evolving cyber threats, ensuring the reliability and safety of future transportation systems.
**Overlap with our engine:** partial — attack impact on cooperative driving; control-theoretic, no full V2X/PKI stack
**Novelty implications:** Does not affect novelty claims of the candidate directions beyond background in platoon/CACC attacks.
