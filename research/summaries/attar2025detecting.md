# Detecting Malicious Artificial Congestion in Connected Cars Environment
Ali El Attar, Mohammad Ali Awali, Rida Khatoun, Makram Hatoum, Khouloud Samrouth · Middle East and North Africa Communications Conference 2025 · https://doi.org/10.1109/menacomm62946.2025.10911022 · relevance: medium · basis: abstract

**Problem:** By 2030, the global connected car market size is projected to be USD 361 billion.
**Approach:** Automakers such as Mercedes-Benz, BMW, Peugeot, Toyota, and others are investing billions of dollars in software development, autonomous driving, and connected vehicle technology. Many companies are developing strong products to secure connected cars and their environment.
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the abstract.
**Results:** We demonstrate, through implementation, the high precision (99%) results obtained with Random Forest, XGBoost, and Support Vector Machines (SVM).
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- However, when a vehicle becomes more connected, it also becomes much more vulnerable to intrusion and cyber-attacks.
- However, the execution time for XGboost seems to be faster than for the other methods.
**Overlap with our engine:** partial — same area (V2X/vehicular security or networking) with a narrower, mechanism-level focus.
**Novelty implications:** Does not remove any of our claims beyond its specific mechanism; we cannot claim to be first on that mechanism. The joint closed-loop traffic/radio/PKI evaluation with counterfactual ground truth remains open as far as this abstract shows.
