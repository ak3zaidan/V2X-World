# A Zero-Trust Approach for Cooperative Vehicular Networks: A Motorway Use Case
Ben Dawson, Hadi Tabatabaee Malazi, Aqeel Kazmi, Saqib Rasool Chaudhry · IEEE Systems, Man, and Cybernetics Magazine 2025 · https://doi.org/10.1109/msmc.2024.3441208 · relevance: medium · basis: abstract

**Problem:** Cooperative vehicular networks, integral to intelligent transportation systems, face vulnerabilities from threat vectors that intentionally mislead information to jeopardize safety.
**Approach:** Existing methods mainly apply trust levels or message comparison/verification based techniques to address the problem. However, implicit authentication models and considering network nodes as trusted are insufficient in protecting network security against invasive and pertinent attacks.
**Tools and scale:** Mentioned in abstract: PKI
**Results:** The evaluation of the proposed technique demonstrates promising results in malicious node detection accuracy, reduced detection delay, and enhanced goodput. Moreover, the good packet delivery ratio of the proposed approach shows 46% and 14% improvements compared to traditional Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) and another trust-based approach.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- However, implicit authentication models and considering network nodes as trusted are insufficient in protecting network security against invasive and pertinent attacks.
- Nevertheless, implementing a ZT in vehicular networks is challenging due to the highly dynamic networking environment, uncertainty in recognizing malicious nodes, and required rapid responses to security breach incidents.
- This article proposes a robust ZT approach addressing the challenges of implementing ZT in vehicular networks.
