# Validating a parking lot assignment method by Eclipse SUMO
Levente Alekszejenkó, Tadeusz Dobrowiecki · SUMO Conference Proceedings 2022 · https://doi.org/10.52825/scp.v2i.93 · relevance: low · basis: abstract

**Problem:** The present paper shows how a novel autonomous vehicle (AV) algorithm can be tested and evaluated by multiple simulations with different levels of refinement.&#x0D; As the first AVs will appear on our roads (possibly) within the upcoming decade, novel traffic problems, like parking lot assignment, will also emerge.
**Approach:** Parking is that kind of activity that will fundamentally change. Today drivers attempt to find parking places close to the destinations to minimize the walking time.
**Tools and scale:** Vehicular setting. The available text does not describe a V2X simulator, radio stack, traffic model or SCMS-style PKI in enough detail to report; scale not stated.
**Results:** Such change will affect human activities, city traffic, and the parking infrastructure.&#x0D; To investigate this problem we need an abstract city model, because an attempt to create a detailed model of the city traffic from the perspective of decades may be misleading and may lead to false emergent conclusions.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- We report on such an abstract model and the obtained conclusions in the paper.&#x0D; A question remains how genuine is the introduced future city model.
**Overlap with our engine:** none: simulation/digital twins in a vehicular context. Low relevance background for the simulator itself; it does not model the joint traffic/radio/SCMS loop, closed-loop revocation or safety impact.
**Novelty implications:** Rules out nothing specific in our candidate directions. Open: the integrated V2X case (SCMS scale, channel load, closed-loop safety impact) is not addressed.
