# Towards a Balance between Privacy and Safety: Microsimulation Framework for Assessing Silence-Based Pseudonym-Change Schemes
Wuping Xin, Hasan M. Moonam, Jonathan Petit, William Whyte · Transportation Research Record: Journal of the Transportation Research Board 2019 · https://doi.org/10.1177/0361198119825833 · relevance: high · basis: abstract

**Problem:** High-frequency awareness messages, such as basic safety messages, in a network of connected vehicles render a continuous stream of location data susceptible to tracking attacks.
**Approach:** As a countermeasure, each vehicle transmits the messages under a regularly changing pseudonym. The pseudonym-change approach is most effective when multiple vehicles change their respective pseudonyms during a collective period of radio silence; this helps obfuscate locations.
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the available text unless given above (topic: pseudonym change/privacy).
**Results:** The results present the network-wide safety impacts and privacy protection effectiveness of the Adaptive Silent Period Strategy, while demonstrating the value of the simulation framework in the design, optimization, and evaluation of silence-based location privacy schemes.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- However, it may compromise safety owing to the missing messages, when silent, defeating the primary goal of connected vehicles enhancing road safety.
**Overlap with our engine:** strong — microsimulation framework measuring privacy vs safety of silence-based pseudonym change; overlaps the safety-cost-of-security direction
**Novelty implications:** Prior work on this exact mechanism; we cannot claim it as new. Open: integration with full radio/traffic stack, per-message ground truth, and safety outcomes in closed loop.
