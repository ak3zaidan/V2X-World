**Approach:** This paper proposes a blockchain‐assisted decentralized authentication and cross‐domain key agreement scheme based on semiconductor superlattice physically unclonable functions (SSL‐PUFs).
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the abstract; appears to be analytical/cryptographic or review work.
**Results:** Security analysis demonstrates resistance against replay, impersonation, man‐in‐the‐middle, physical cloning, and single‐point compromise attacks. Experimental results show that the proposed scheme requires only 512 bytes per authentication session, reducing communication overhead by 50.0%–74.2% compared with recent PUF‐based protocols.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- Vehicular ad hoc networks (VANETs) enable secure vehicle‐to‐vehicle (V2V) and vehicle‐to‐infrastructure (V2I) communications but still rely heavily on centralized trusted authorities, creating scalability limitations, single‐point failures, and increased authentication latency.
- The proposed framework replaces centralized challenge–response pair (CRP) storage with blockchain‐maintained cryptographic commitments verified through consortium roadside unit (RSU) validators.
