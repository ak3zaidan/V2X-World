# Addressing Spatially Structured Interference in Causal Analysis Using Propensity Scores
Keith W. Zirkle, Marie-Abele Bind, Jenise L. Swall et al. · arXiv 2021 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.09297 · relevance: low · basis: abstract

**Problem:** Environmental epidemiologists are increasingly interested in establishing causality between exposures and health outcomes.
**Approach:** A popular model for causal inference is the Rubin Causal Model (RCM), which typically seeks to estimate the average difference in study units' potential outcomes.
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the abstract.
**Results:** We use propensity score-based methods to estimate these effects when considering the effects of the Environmental Protection Agency's 2005 nonattainment designations for particulate matter with aerodynamic diameter less than 2.5 micrograms per cubic meter (PM2.5) on lung cancer incidence using county-level data obtained from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) Program.
