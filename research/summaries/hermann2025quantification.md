# Quantification Methods for Trust in Cooperative Driving
Artur Hermann, Nataša Trkulja, Patrick Wachter, Benjamin Erb, Frank Kargl · IEEE Vehicular Networking Conference (VNC) 2025 · https://doi.org/10.1109/vnc64509.2025.11054209 · relevance: medium · basis: abstract

**Problem:** Future vehicles and infrastructure will rely on data from external entities such as other vehicles via V2X communication for safety-critical applications.
**Approach:** Malicious manipulation of this data can lead to safety incidents. Earlier works proposed a trust assessment framework (TAF) to allow a vehicle or infrastructure node to assess whether it can trust the data it received.
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the abstract.
**Results:** Our evaluations in a smart traffic light system scenario show that the TAF detects attacks with an accuracy greater than 96% and intersection throughput increased by 42% while maintaining safety and security, when using our proposed quantification methods.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- Future vehicles and infrastructure will rely on data from external entities such as other vehicles via V2X communication for safety-critical applications.
- One particular challenge in trust assessment is the appropriate quantification of this evidence.
**Overlap with our engine:** partial — same area (V2X/vehicular security or networking) with a narrower, mechanism-level focus.
**Novelty implications:** Does not remove any of our claims beyond its specific mechanism; we cannot claim to be first on that mechanism. The joint closed-loop traffic/radio/PKI evaluation with counterfactual ground truth remains open as far as this abstract shows.
