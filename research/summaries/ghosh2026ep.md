# EP-CF: Epoch-Partitioned Cuckoo Filters for Low-Latency, Bounded-Memory Certificate Revocation Checking in Resource-Constrained IoT Gateways
Aritrik Ghosh · venue not stated 2026 · https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7237819 · relevance: low · basis: abstract

**Problem:** Certificate-based authentication is the de facto trust mechanism for constrained Internet of Things (IoT) deployments, yet the standard revocation-checking primitives inherited from web PKI-Certificate Revocation Lists (CRLs) and the Online Certificate Status Protocol (OCSP)-are poorly matched to edge gateways that must validate large numbers of device certificates under tight memory and energy budgets.
**Approach:** Certificate-based authentication is the de facto trust mechanism for constrained Internet of Things (IoT) deployments, yet the standard revocation-checking primitives inherited from web PKI-Certificate Revocation Lists (CRLs) and the Online Certificate Status Protocol (OCSP)-are poorly matched to edge gateways that must validate large numbers of device certificates under tight memory and energy budgets.
**Tools and scale:** In our simulation harness, EP-CF reduces amortized rebuild-related CPU cost by over an order of magnitude relative to monolithic filters, while per-operation query and update latency remain within a small constant factor of the monolithic baselines, at the cost of a modest, bounded increase in per-query filter checks and a correspondingly modest increase in realized false-positive rate.
**Results:** We implement EP-CF and three baselines (flat CRL, monolithic Bloom filter, monolithic Cuckoo filter) in Python, evaluate them on certificate-serial workloads derived from Certificate Transparency-style revocation distributions at scales from 50 k to 10 M certificates, and report false-positive rate, query latency, update latency, and steady-state memory with rigorous statistical analysis (n = 30 independent runs, 95% confidence intervals).
