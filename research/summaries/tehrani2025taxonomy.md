# A Taxonomy of System-Level Attacks on Deep Learning Models in Autonomous Vehicles
Masoud Jamshidiyan Tehrani, Jin-Han Kim, Rosmaël Zidane Lekeufack Foulefack, Alessandro Marchetto, Paolo Tonella · ACM Transactions on Software Engineering and Methodology 2025 · https://doi.org/10.1145/3769009 · relevance: medium · basis: abstract

**Problem:** The advent of deep learning and its astonishing performance has enabled its usage in complex systems, including autonomous vehicles.
**Approach:** On the other hand, deep learning models are susceptible to mis-predictions when small, adversarial changes are introduced into their input. Such mis-predictions can be triggered in the real world and can result in a failure of the entire system.
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the abstract.
**Results:** Such mis-predictions can be triggered in the real world and can result in a failure of the entire system.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- There has been however no systematic attempt to analyze and categorize such attacks.
- We distilled several lessons for practitioners and identified possible directions for future work for researchers.
**Overlap with our engine:** partial — same area (V2X/vehicular security or networking) with a narrower, mechanism-level focus.
**Novelty implications:** Does not remove any of our claims beyond its specific mechanism; we cannot claim to be first on that mechanism. The joint closed-loop traffic/radio/PKI evaluation with counterfactual ground truth remains open as far as this abstract shows.
