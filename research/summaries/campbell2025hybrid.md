# Hybrid Post-Quantum Signatures for Bitcoin and Ethereum: A Protocol-Level Integration Strategy
Robert Campbell · The Journal of The British Blockchain Association 2025 · https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202509.2079.v1 · relevance: low · basis: abstract

**Problem:** The transition to post-quantum cryptography poses an unprecedented challenge for Bitcoin and Ethereum, as it involves implementing a defensive downgrade that imposes immediate, severe costs with no tangible benefits.
**Approach:** This paper presents a comprehensive framework acknowledging these harsh realities. While we propose specific BIP/EIP implementations and optimization strategies that might achieve 50–60% capacity retention, we recognize that the quantum threat timeline may now be shorter than even the minimum viable migration period.
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the abstract beyond the above; no traffic simulator, radio stack or SCMS-scale deployment is described.
**Results:** While quantum computers capable of breaking secp256k1 require between 523–2,500 logical qubits, with the author deriving 523 logical qubits as an algorithmic lower bound (not inclusive of arithmetic and ancilla qubits) for a canonical Shor/phase-estimation circuit using the formula QL = 2⌈log2(n)⌉ + 2 + ⌈log2(2 + 1/(2ε))⌉ for ε = 0.001, and conservative estimates ranging up to 2,500 logical qubits based on comprehensive resource models—significantly less than the 2,100–2,400 logical qubits es- timated for general elliptic curves—current systems achieve only ∼100 logical qubits. IBM’s quantum roadmap projects 500–1,000 logical qubits by 2029, placing the critical threshold within 4–10 years depending on which estimate proves accurate.
