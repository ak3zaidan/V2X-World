# LitCVit: A Lightweight Self-Supervised Contrastive Vision Transformer for Encrypted Malicious Traffic Detection
M. Nisa, Adnan Noor Mian, M. Rehmani · IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security 2026 · https://doi.org/10.1109/tifs.2026.3683528 · relevance: low · basis: abstract

**Problem:** Malicious traffic detection often requires large, labeled datasets, which are challenging due to privacy concerns, labeling costs, and evolving threat patterns.
**Approach:** To address these issues, we propose LitCVit, a lightweight self-supervised contrastive Vision Transformer-based framework that captures cross-flow semantic and behavioral patterns to generate robust latent representations of encrypted traffic. Without relying on decryption or manually engineered features, our method enables efficient detection of encrypted malicious flows with low inference time.
**Tools and scale:** Not stated in the abstract.
**Results:** Extensive evaluations on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves an average detection accuracy of 98.10% and F1-score of 98.08%. Compared to the best state-of-the-art model, LitCVit achieves an average improvement of 2.49% in F1-score, 2.12% in precision, and 2.50% in recall, highlighting its superior detection capability in encrypted traffic scenarios.
