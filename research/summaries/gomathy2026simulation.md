**Approach:** The very dynamic and open design of Vehicular Ad Hoc Networks (VANETs) makes them particularly vulnerable to identity-based attacks, and in particular Sybil attacks, where rogue vehicles introduce fake traffic packets through a myriad of counterfeit identities.
**Tools and scale:** The simulation platform running on MATLAB was designed in a manner that it evaluated the security and performance under the impact of malicious Sybil nodes that did not send consistent traffic messages.
**Results:** Vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communication is the foundation of the cooperative and autonomous driving frameworks in modern vehicular networks, which requires high levels of message authentication and trusting with strict real-time constraints. This paper responds to these threats by the provision of a lean lightweight, blockchain-enhanced trust-management architecture that is tailored to V2X use cases.
