# Implementation And Evaluation Of Random Forest, XGBoost Algorithms And Trust Management Based On Simple Blockchain For DDoS Attack Detection In Vanet Using Simulation-Based Data
Mahardika Wisnu Aji, Hendrawan, Nana Rachmana Syambas, Galih Nugraha Nurkahfi · 2025 19th International Conference on Telecommunication Systems, Services, and Applications (TSSA) 2025 · https://doi.org/10.1109/tssa68467.2025.11303841 · relevance: low · basis: abstract

**Problem:** This paper proposes an integrated DDoS detection system for Vehicular Ad-hoc Networks (VANET), combining blockchain-based trust management with Random Forest and XGBoost to enhance detection accuracy.
**Approach:** The system is built and evaluated on a controlled simulation dataset generated by our traffic FCD generator and an IEEE 802.11bdbased VANET communication simulator, restricted to a six-lane divided highway (3 lanes per direction); no intersections or ramps are modeled. The dataset spans multiple mobility patterns and traffic densities (very-light to gridlock) with three attack types (Flooding, High-Power Jamming, and Synchronize).
**Tools and scale:** Vehicular setting. The available text does not describe a V2X simulator, radio stack, traffic model or SCMS-style PKI in enough detail to report; scale not stated.
**Results:** The system is built and evaluated on a controlled simulation dataset generated by our traffic FCD generator and an IEEE 802.11bdbased VANET communication simulator, restricted to a six-lane divided highway (3 lanes per direction); no intersections or ramps are modeled. As VANETs—also supported by 5G backhaul—are prone to DDoS that disrupt safety beacons, traditional thresholding is insufficient.
**Limitations and future work (as stated by the authors):**
- Using simulation data provides repeatability, precise control, and clean ground truth while covering edge cases that are hard or unsafe to collect on-road; future work will extend beyond the six-lane highway to urban grids and mixed road classes for external validation.
