# Hostile review C: "Two providers, one fleet" (cross-provider MA cooperation + CTL change propagation)

Reviewer role: PC member, **ISOC VehicleSec** (co-located with NDSS). I chose it because the work is a policy-and-measurement study of a deployed vehicular PKI with a simulator as the instrument. It is too narrow and too analytical for USENIX/CCS, and PoPETs does not fit because the contribution is not about privacy. IEEE VNC would be the fallback.

Sources I checked myself are cited inline. I did not open research/synthesis, research/explore (other than writing this file) or research/scrutiny.

---

## 1. Summary of the submission as pitched

The production US CTL lists more than one SCMS provider. The pitch argues three things. A reporter's MA cannot resolve another provider's linkage values. The draft 5GAA policy says only that MAs "should" cooperate. The CTL Update Process is unpublished. From this it proposes to measure four things: how an attacker's evidence splits across MAs; time to revocation and honest-revocation rate under no, delayed and immediate cooperation, at 50/50, 80/20 and 95/5 shares; a forum-shopping attacker who enrols with the most lenient provider; and, optionally, how many messages go unverifiable while a CTL addition or removal propagates.

## 2. Does it matter? Who would change what?

**Partly.** The governance gap is real but smaller than the pitch claims.

- The production CTL of 13 Aug 2025 has exactly two roots: ISS/OmniTrust and SAESOL S2X (repo doc `docs/design/scms-real/01-deployment-and-governance.md` item 2, from the CTL file itself). The 2025 test CTL has 5–6 roots (same doc). The USDOT Aug 2024 plan sets a non-binding target that "2 SCMS providers demonstrate interoperable security credentials management" by 2028 (same doc, item 10, citing USDOT plan pp.10–13). The multi-provider setting is therefore real.
- **The premise that cooperation is only "should" is wrong for the binding document.** SCMS Manager *Provider Requirements* v1.1 (9 Aug 2024), §2.2, p.9, says: "Providers shall implement a Misbehavior Authority (MA) component … The Provider SHALL agree to the cooperative sharing of misbehavior reports with other SCMS Manager Providers" (https://www.scmsmanager.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/SCMS-Manager-Provider-Requirements-v1_1.pdf, p.9). The 5GAA draft North-American CP also has a "shall": "(13) An SCMS Provider shall cooperate with MAs" (5GAA TR "Credential Management Supporting V2X Commercial Deployments" v1.0, 2025, p.21). Only items (24)–(26) on p.22 use "should": "An MA should cooperate with all SCMS Providers published on the CTL", "should provide linking information", "should support the end entity revocation requests from other MAs" (https://5gaa.org/content/uploads/2025/07/credential-management-supporting-v2x-commercial-deployments.pdf, p.22). The binding US text already mandates sharing. What it lacks is a **deadline, format and resolution protocol** for sharing. The pitch's headline "is a binding cooperation deadline needed?" therefore shrinks to "what should the SLA number be?" That is still a legitimate question, but a much smaller one, and the paper must not frame it as "cooperation is optional".
- **Is anyone positioned to change something?** In principle, yes: the SCMS Manager could add a forwarding SLA to Provider Requirements §2.2, and 5GAA could harden (24)–(26). In practice, production MA operation at scale is unconfirmed. The repo's own validation doc records that an end-to-end over-the-air MBD-to-CRL demo was first shown only at a June 2026 plugfest (`01-deployment-and-governance.md` item 7). A Phase-2 CV-Pilot document says the SCMS "does not currently have a standard Misbehavior Detection and … CRL distribution mechanism" (`03-protocols-and-operations.md` §8, row "Pilot gap", citing CVP-P2 item 21). Policy authors will ask you to tune the SLA of a pipeline that barely exists. That makes the work timely, but it also means none of the per-provider MA settings you vary can be anchored to reality.

## 3. Novelty (my own search)

- **arXiv** (export API: `all:"misbehavior authority"`, `abs:"multiple SCMS"`, `abs:misbehavior AND revocation AND V2X`) returns only Blackchain (arXiv 1710.08891) as on-topic. Blackchain motivates multiple competing SCMSs and a distributed set of MAs, but it is conceptual and has no evaluation (corpus `heijden2017blackchain`; DOI 10.1145/3152824.3152828).
- **The SCMS design paper** (Brecht et al., arXiv 1802.05323) covers elector-based root management and the CTL architecturally. It notes that out-of-band root installation is suboptimal because parts of the system stay "out of order" until every device has the root. That is a qualitative statement of exactly the CTL-propagation problem, with no measurement.
- **SECMACE** (Khodaei, Jin, Papadimitratos, IEEE T-ITS, DOI 10.1109/tits.2017.2722688; corpus `khodaei2017secmace`, PDF pp.1–4) is the omitted prior art. It is an implemented multi-domain VPKI with home and foreign LTCAs and cross-domain pseudonym resolution, evaluated for performance. It does not measure split misbehaviour evidence, but any claim that multi-provider VPKI is treated "only architecturally" must cite it and say how this work differs.
- **5GAA "Misbehaviour Detection" white paper (2022)**, p.27, lists as open questions: "How can the free-rider problem be avoided? For any given OEM the incentive is for their vehicles not to report …" and "Can a vehicle OEM manipulate reporting by only reporting other OEM vehicles, or by applying different thresholds to different OEM vehicles?" (https://5gaa.org/content/uploads/2022/07/5GAA-Misbehaviour-detection-Final.pdf, p.27). Industry has already named the multi-party incentive and threshold-asymmetry problem, which is close to the "lenient provider" arm. That supports relevance but weakens any "first to identify" claim. It is an open question, not a measured one.
- **Other searches.** The Stan4CRA EN 304 624 draft says MA services are "not yet fully define[d] and standardized" (ETSI labs commit pages, found via web search). A web search for CTL/ECTL propagation-latency studies returned nothing. Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex were rate-limited for me, so their coverage is **not** checked.
- **Verdict on novelty.** I found no paper that measures cross-provider MA evidence splitting or CTL propagation exposure. Novelty of the *measurement* is plausible. Novelty of the *problem statement* is weak: Blackchain, Brecht et al., SECMACE and 5GAA 2022 all name it.

## 4. Is the threat model or premise sound?

The pitch has four premise problems. A hostile reviewer would raise all of them.

1. **The split is a routing artefact, not a cryptographic necessity.** A US MBR carries the suspect's `signingCertificate` in full (`SignedBsmsWithCertificate`, SCMS Manager MBR spec v1.0 / ASN.1 v1.1, mirrored in `crates/v2xw-proto/src/scms/mbr.rs` lines 1–20). Any receiving MA can read the issuer chain and identify the responsible provider at once. "MA A cannot resolve B's linkage values" is true but beside the point, because A only has to forward. Since §2.2 already says SHALL share, the realistic "no cooperation" arm describes a non-compliant provider. The defensible framing is **forwarding latency and batching**, plus **who decides** when evidence arrives from several sources.
2. **The timescale does not match what the engine measures.** In the US flow, a report is POSTed to the reporter's RA, which shuffles reports with a threshold of 10,000 reports or 1 day (CAMP SCMS 1.2.2 SCMS-765, recorded in `03-protocols-and-operations.md` line 43 and line 356). The on-vehicle upload queue decays report weights daily (SM-MBR §1–4, same line). CRL publication is allowed up to 1 business day or 24 h (Provider Req. §3.4.10; 5GAA (254)–(255), recorded in `03-protocols-and-operations.md` lines 42 and 340). Time to revocation is therefore dominated by **day-scale policy queues**. The engine's strengths, sub-second radio survival and witness geometry, change *how many* reports exist but barely affect *when* revocation happens. An extra cross-provider forwarding delay of d simply adds d. Measuring an additive constant on a multi-day pipeline does not need a city-scale closed loop.
3. **The evidence split is analytically trivial unless provider membership is spatially correlated.** If provider assignment is independent of position, each report reaches the subject's provider's MA with probability equal to that provider's share s, which is binomial thinning. Time to the k-th report then scales roughly as 1/s, and honest-revocation (false-positive) rates fall the same way. The pitch admits "the split share alone is analytical". The engine adds value only if reporter density, report survival or provider share varies across space and time in a way that breaks independence, for example fleets or brands clustered by route or depot. The pitch does not hypothesise such a correlation, so the expected result is the closed form plus noise.
4. **Forum shopping is real, but cooperation does not fix it.** Only the subject's provider can resolve its own linkage values, so the lenient provider's MA makes the revocation decision however quickly evidence reaches it. Cooperation timing therefore cannot cure leniency; only uniform MA decision policy can. A study that varies both factors will find that leniency dominates. That finding is a governance argument that needs no simulator. Also, whether an attacker can choose a provider depends on who provisions the OBU (an OEM contract versus an aftermarket device), and the pitch does not model that.

**CTL part.** The premise holds: the CTL Update Process is listed as "in development" (Provider Req. §1.1, recorded in `01-deployment-and-governance.md` line 83). Existing timing rules are a root addition published within 3 business days (Provider Req. §3.4.11) and, in the 5GAA draft, ≤15 days after an addition and ≤24 h after a removal (`03-protocols-and-operations.md` line 346). The exposure, however, equals the distribution of vehicle CTL-refresh times multiplied by encounter rates. The refresh distribution is an OEM-specific unknown that you would *assume*, not measure. One useful point the pitch misses is that the engine decodes a **composite CRL that carries the CTL** (`crates/v2xw-sec/src/ctl.rs` `decode_composite_crl`, line 357). If deployed CTLs ride the CRL channel, CTL propagation inherits CRL-download behaviour, which the engine already records (`MaCrlDownload`, `crates/v2xw-record/src/dataset/tables.rs` line 372). That is the strongest engine hook in the whole pitch, and the pitch marks it "optional".

## 5. Would simulation evidence convince me? What validation would I require?

Not as pitched. My requirements:

- **Engine capability is not yet there.** The MA in the engine is one pipeline: `threat/ma/legacy-window`, with k = 3 trusted reporters over a 15 s window (`crates/v2xw-threat/src/ma.rs` lines 1–5). It has no per-provider MA instances, no per-vehicle provider tag and no MA-to-MA forwarding. Resolution models only the LA1/LA2 split versus a single authority (`crates/v2xw-threat/src/resolve.rs` lines 7–16, 140–167). The trust store installs CTL roots at start (`crates/v2xw-node/src/rxval.rs` lines 520, 581–582), and multi-day persistence is missing (the pitch says so). "A few days" for per-provider MA settings is optimistic once you count validation.
- **The 15 s window clashes with the 1-day RA shuffle.** If the MA decision rule is a 15 s sliding window, a report delayed by hours never contributes, so even "immediate" cooperation fails under realistic transport. You must either model an MA with a day-scale evidence horizon, which has no public US reference, or show that results do not depend on the MA design. Without that, every curve is an artefact of the chosen MA.
- **Ground the MA settings.** Real MA thresholds are not public. Anchor the "lenient" and "strict" settings to something citable, such as SAE J3287 or the ETSI TS 103 759 / TR 103 460 decision structure, or present them explicitly as a sensitivity sweep and report breakpoints, not point estimates.
- **Validate against the closed form.** Under independent provider assignment, show the engine reproduces binomial thinning (time to k reports ∝ 1/s) within confidence intervals. Then show *where and why* it departs. A departure with a named mechanism is the only result the engine uniquely contributes.
- **Inject a fault.** Assign all reporters to one provider and the attacker to the other with cooperation off, and show that revocation goes to zero. This proves the cooperation switch actually does something.
- **CTL part.** The refresh-time distribution must be sourced, for example from CRL-fetch cadence rules in the CAMP or 5GAA documents, or swept. Report the result as lead time versus refresh percentile.

## 6. Strongest reasons to reject

1. **A false or overstated premise.** Binding Provider Requirements §2.2 already says "SHALL agree to the cooperative sharing of misbehavior reports" (p.9), and 5GAA (13) says "shall cooperate" (p.21). The "only 'should'" framing comes from cherry-picking 5GAA (24)–(26).
2. **The headline results are predictable in closed form.** They are thinning by market share plus an additive forwarding delay, and leniency dominates cooperation. The simulator adds geometry noise to an analytical answer.
3. **The timescales do not match.** Revocation latency is dominated by day-scale shuffles and SLAs, while the engine's fidelity is sub-second.
4. **The MA policies have no ground truth.** Per-provider MA settings are invented, and real US MA operation is unconfirmed.
5. **Prior art is missing.** SECMACE (multi-domain VPKI) and 5GAA 2022 p.27 (multi-OEM threshold manipulation) are absent from the related-work claim.
6. **Thesis fit is poor.** The work uses the engine's closed loop only marginally, which is the opposite of what the user asked for. The relayed user request wants the engine used and novelty that is "worthwhile".

## 7. What would make it an accept?

- **Reframe.** Drop "cooperation is optional". Ask instead: "Provider Requirements §2.2 mandates sharing without a deadline or decision protocol. What SLA and what cross-MA decision rule keep revocation time and false revocation within target, given real report transport (upload queues, 1-day shuffle, CRL SLA)?" Recommend a concrete clause text for §2.2 and 5GAA (24)–(26).
- **Make the engine indispensable.** Find and show a mechanism that breaks independence: spatial or temporal clustering of provider share (OEM fleets, transit and DOT RSUs on one provider), RSUs as reporters (RSUs can be on a different provider than most OBUs), or report survival that differs by provider because of OBU class. The paper is publishable only if the engine result departs from the closed form for a named reason.
- **Make the CTL arm the main contribution, not an option.** Use the composite-CRL-carries-CTL path and the existing CRL-download records. Measure per-encounter verification failure after a root addition or removal, including the removal case where vehicles keep trusting a removed root. Turn it into a lead-time recommendation for the unpublished CTL Update Process. This is the more novel half: I found nothing measured, and Brecht et al. (arXiv 1802.05323) state the problem only qualitatively.
- **Add an adversarial multi-provider attack the closed form cannot predict**, for example a colluding reporter coalition all enrolled at one provider whose fabricated reports, forwarded to the victim's provider, bypass that MA's reporter-reputation gate because the forwarding MA strips or launders reporter identity. This tests whether cooperation *creates* a framing channel (the reputation gate is in `crates/v2xw-threat/src/ma.rs` lines 16–21). That would be a genuinely new security result, not a measurement of an obvious trade-off.
- **Sensitivity over MA designs**, with all parameters cited or swept.

## 8. Score

**Reject** for VehicleSec in its pitched form. **Confidence: 4/5.**

If the reframing in §7 were done, with the CTL arm as the main contribution, the cross-MA framing channel added and the closed-form validation shown, I would expect **Major revision**, trending to Accept at VehicleSec or VNC. As a thesis anchor it is weak. As one chapter that answers "what lead time and what SLA" with engine evidence, it is reasonable.
