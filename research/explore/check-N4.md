# Check N4: the compromised intersection (RSU that lies, filters reports, relays the CRL)

Blind check, 2026-10-18. Sources: corpus (catalog ids), web, standards drafts, and engine code read with grep/sed. I opened nothing under research/synthesis/ or research/explore/ except THOUGHTS.md.

## Verdict: PARTLY DONE in pieces. The combined study is novel, but two of its three premises are wrong for the US system.

No paper, thesis, preprint, white paper or patent I found measures the end-to-end loop. That loop runs from a compromised RSU signing false SPaT/MAP, through vehicles detecting and reporting it, to the report path running through that same RSU, and on to the SCMS neutralising the RSU, with and without an independent (cellular) path. Each piece exists on its own (table below). Before any engine work, though, the pitch has to be repaired on three points. Each is cited in §2.

1. **"Filters the reports against it" is not possible as stated.** A US misbehaviour report is signed, **encrypted to the MA certificate**, and POSTed to the RA (`/process-misbehavior-report`). The RSU only routes IP traffic and cannot read the subject (docs/design/scms-real/03-protocols-and-operations.md item 9 and the endpoint table; both cite SCMS Manager MBR v1.0/ASN v1.1). It can only drop *all* uplink, perhaps guided by timing. A blanket drop shows up at the vehicle as a failed HTTP POST, and the vehicle keeps reports it has not sent for up to a week (CAMP-EE §2.2.8, quoted at crates/v2xw-node/src/rsu.rs:502-506). So suppression becomes **delay until the next independent uplink**, not loss.
2. **"Relays the CRL that should revoke it" is largely moot.** In the US design an RSU is not revoked by CRL in practice. Its application certificate lasts 169 h (CAMP Table 68, cited at crates/v2xw-proto/src/scms/appcert.rs:6) and is renewed weekly over the RSU's backhaul. The THEA pilot's security concept says: *"RSUs will have application certificates with short validity periods (e.g., daily, hourly) … and hence no RSU CRL is necessary. … the TMC should be able to provide sufficient monitoring to determine if a RSU … has been compromised"* (FHWA-JPO-18-693, Dec 2018, p.18, https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/42690). Wyoming's: *"only OBEs will use the CRL. Misbehaving RSEs will be powered down and replaced"* (FHWA-JPO-16-288, Mar 2016, p.23, https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/30810). The CI security draft likewise relies on blocking credential re-issuance (CTI 4501/3 PropDft JAN2026 §6.3.4.2.18, p.36). A series-3 hash CRL for RSU certificates does exist in 1609.2 (appcert.rs:13), but neutralisation in practice means the RA refusing renewal plus the operator unplugging the unit.
3. **"Delay its own revocation indefinitely in sparse areas" is true by construction.** If every report must cross the accused RSU and it drops them, the result follows with no experiment, and reviewers will say so. It is also contradicted by store-and-forward (point 1). The only non-trivial quantity is the **incidental-bypass rate and delay**: how fast reports escape through cellular-equipped vehicles, neighbouring RSUs and held-report retry. Even that is bounded by the operator path: the CI draft lists cameras, a public phone line, **or** vehicle misbehaviour detection as acceptable ways to detect inconsistent SPaT (CTI 4501/3 §6.3.4.7.3, p.41).

**What remains open and is worth doing (narrower):** CTI 4501/3 §6.3.4.7.3 names "autonomous misbehavior detection carried out by approaching vehicles and reported to an appropriate authority" as one way to meet the requirement. It then says *"'timely' is deliberately not precisely defined"* (p.41). Nobody has quantified what timeliness the vehicle-report path actually gives. That depends on RSU density, cellular penetration, report retention and retry, the RA's report shuffle (threshold of 10,000 reports or 1 day, CAMP122 §5.2.8.4 via 03-protocols item 9), the MA decision rule and the 169 h certificate. The open question is how that compares with operator monitoring, and how much a compromised RSU that blocks the local uplink stretches it. Two more facts sharpen it. No standard SPaT misbehaviour report type exists: the CI draft refers only to "the BSM misbehaviors defined in SAE J3287" (§7.3.4.4.4, p.85), and C2C-CC says *"an assessment of the efficiency and robustness of the proposed detection mechanisms for RSUs is missing"* (WP 2092 v1.0, 2021, pp.23-24). And CTI 4501/3 is still a proposed draft (Jan 2026), so a quantified "timely" could feed its ballot. This is a solid workshop or short-journal result and a thesis section, but a smaller contribution than the pitch claims.

## 2. Engine feasibility

**Already exists:**
- Compromised-RSU attacker: `crates/v2xw-threat/src/attack_rsu.rs`. `RsuAttackKind` = FalseSpat, FalseMap, FalseCrl, FalseCtl, SuppressForwardedReports, PoisonForwardedReports (l.60-95). `on_forward` drops with `suppress_prob` (default 1.0), **regardless of subject** (l.377-400). There is **one kind per unit**, so the unit cannot lie and drop at the same time.
- RSU runtime in the Compromised state suppresses **all** forwarding and **all** broadcasts (`crates/v2xw-node/src/rsu.rs:1252-1263, 1611-1615`). A compromised RSU therefore currently sends *no* SPaT; it does not send false SPaT. The module's own doc says the falsification is "declared … not yet felt" (attack_rsu.rs:14-23).
- Report forwarding through the RSU, with the attacker deciding: `crates/v2xw-engine/src/run.rs:~7170-7215` (`rsu_forward`, role `report-forward`).
- Per-vehicle access: Cellular, RsuRelay or Offline, with a penetration draw (`crates/v2xw-engine/src/backend.rs:1059-1080`). A cellular vehicle with no coverage falls back to the RSU relay, and reports are held store-and-forward (run.rs:8225-8320, `phase2.rs:3476-3505`). The relay is **fire-and-forget with no end-to-end acknowledgement** (run.rs doc above `relay_report`, l.8320-8327). Real uploads are HTTP POSTs to the RA.
- CRL paths: RSU CrlDistribution role (rsu.rs:109-111) and cellular CRL polls (run.rs:7896; phase2.rs:345).
- RSU application certificates: 169 h validity, weekly renewal over the backhaul, RA refusal "blocklisted", starvation computed exactly (`crates/v2xw-proto/src/scms/appcert.rs:1-15`, `run/appcert.rs:1-30, 971`).
- GLOSA and RLVW already consume the **received** SPaT/MAP (`crates/v2xw-node/src/apps.rs:942` `on_spat`, `:1222` `signal_situation`), and GLOSA compliance changes speed (run.rs:288-396). The pitch's "phase 2" is therefore mostly built already. What is missing is a falsified SPaT to consume.

**Must be built (rough size; about 1.4-2.5 k LOC, 2-4 weeks, not 1-2):**
1. A compromised RSU that signs and broadcasts *edited* SPaT/MAP (apply `InfraClaim` before signing) instead of going silent: about 200-400 LOC.
2. A composite attacker (lie and drop at once). Suppression must be blind to the report subject, because reports are encrypted to the MA; `on_forward` currently reads `subject_cert_digest` in the clear. Optionally a timing-based heuristic. About 100-250 LOC.
3. A vehicle-side SPaT detector: the engine has no signal-state belief (`crates/v2xw-threat/src/ts103759.rs:1374-1378` says a `LocalSignalState` trait is needed). It also needs a SPaT report type, which has no standard format. About 400-800 LOC.
4. An MA path for an infrastructure subject: map RSU certificate to device, a decision rule, then RA blocklist leading to renewal refusal and optionally a series-3 hash-CRL entry. `crlg.hash_entries` exists (`run/mod.rs:698`) but **nothing writes to it**, and the MA revocation flow is linkage-only (`run/revoke.rs`). About 300-600 LOC.
5. End-to-end acknowledgement and retry for relayed reports (HTTP POST realism). Without it the engine overstates suppression. About 150-300 LOC.
6. Metrics: share suppressed, bypass rate and delay, vehicles exposed before neutralisation. About 200 LOC.

**Engine limits that undermine it (THOUGHTS.md):**
- **Time scale.** Neutralisation is set by the RA shuffle (up to 1 day), the MA decision and the 169 h certificate, so it takes days. Runs are about 600 s (about 2.4 h of wall time at ~450 vehicles). The engine can measure report generation, bypass rates and exposure per hour of traffic. The time to neutralise has to be extrapolated with a renewal or queueing model, so the headline number is analytical with engine-fed rates. This is the same "purely analytical core" weakness that parked harvest-now-link-later.
- **Harm.** "V2X warnings do not yet change driving (only GLOSA and platooning close the loop)." False SPaT can therefore only bend GLOSA speeds. RLVW warnings do not stop drivers, so the engine cannot show red-light-running or crash harm without new driver-response work.
- **Counterfactuals.** RNG streams are keyed per entity, so cellular-versus-relay comparisons are clean only where the change is backend-only. A different penetration changes which vehicles are cellular through per-node draws, which is stable, but closed-loop GLOSA effects diverge.

## 3. Bottom line

Do not pursue the pitch as written. Two of its three attack surfaces are refuted by the US architecture (encrypted reports; RSUs neutralised by non-renewal and operator action, not by CRL), and its headline is tautological. A defensible reframing is: **"How timely is vehicle-reported detection of a misbehaving connected intersection?"** It quantifies the undefined "timely" in CTI 4501/3 §6.3.4.7.3 across RSU density, cellular penetration, retry semantics and the 169 h renewal cycle, and adds a compromised RSU that blocks local uplink. It is novel and adoptable for the open CTI 4501/3 draft, but modest in size and mostly analytical over days, with the engine supplying rates. It fits as a chapter or short paper, not as the thesis anchor.
