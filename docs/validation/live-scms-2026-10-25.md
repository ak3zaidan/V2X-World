# Live SCMS knob tests, 2026-10-25 (continued 2026-10-26)

The live engine track for the SCMS settings. Each knob of
`docs/guides/config-inventory-scms.md` is changed alone on the same seed. Its effect on the
SCMS metrics, the backend loads, the on-air sizes and channel load, and the run cost was
predicted before the run, then measured.

- **Base** `scenarios/live-scms/base.yaml`: `credential-lifecycle.yaml` cut to 180 s.
  It has a compressed calendar, 60 s i-periods, 5 certificates a period, 2 preloaded, a
  30 s report shuffle, an automatic MA, a CRL every 30 s, 1,500 veh/h, 72 devices and
  5 % ConstPos attackers from 60 s. There are 42 one-change variants beside it, one file
  each. Every file validates.
- **Binary**: the integrator's release `v2xw` built from `486c8cba`, copied to the
  scratchpad. Runs used `--record-level events`, one run per run-lock call (second slot,
  1,200 MB cap). L ran in 49 s at 67 MB.
- **Rows**: `crates/v2xw-engine/tests/expectations/live-scms.yaml` has 108 rows, committed
  with `measured: pending` (`abe76a80`) before any variant ran. Three predictions were
  corrected on the base's numbers before their own runs (`123f519d`, each with a note):
  - PP4: a top-up due at the run's last instant is still started.
  - RW0, RW10: the revocation latency is quantised by the 30 s CRL slot.
- **Measures**: `tools/realworld/matrix.py extract`, which reads the run report, the
  metrics and the PKI-flow records. `tools/realworld/live_scms.py extend` adds provider
  shares, latency in seconds, airtime per frame, CRL polls, the unknown-signer loss, and
  backend link messages and bytes per entity.
- **Faults**: `python3 -I tools/realworld/live_scms.py faults <table> <runs>` puts each
  row's fault into the measured values and re-judges with matrix.py's own `judge`. The
  fault is either "the knob is ignored" (the variant's measures replaced by the base's) or
  "the value moved past the row's bound". All 40 judged rows go red; none stays green.
- **Determinism**: L and L2 (same seed, same build stamp) have byte-identical records and
  metrics.

### The three engine tests that fail on main

All three were reproduced with the release binary on scenario files equal to the tests'
(`scmsknobs/sc/*.yaml`: same numbers to the millisecond and the frame). They have one root
in common: since `0d62f322` (acagroup, PV.aca-cannot-group) the RA sends one
RaAcaCertRequestSpdu per certificate, shuffled across a population batch, and the weekly
job for a period already due runs lazily, at the first device's `.info` request after the
boundary, telling that device `nextDlTime = now + first_batch_delay`.

1. **`scms_knobs_runs::a_longer_download_wait_lengthens_a_late_topup`** (19,182 against
   20,873 ms; the test needs +5,000). The `slow` fault charges its 4 s on every message the
   ACA handles. Per-certificate messages turn "the ACA 4 s late" into a queue: top-up
   latency mean 19.2 s, max 134 s, 112 of 217 top-ups complete in 300 s. Most batches are
   ready at the first poll, so the poll interval only acts on the late minority: +1.7 s
   is about what a 2 -> 20 s wait gives a ~20 % late share (+9 s each). The test's premise
   (every batch about 4 s late) no longer holds.
2. **`sensitivity_security::the_backhaul_latency_and_bandwidth_move_relay_topups`**
   (128 kbit/s 1,690 ms against 200 ms 2,023 ms). The lazy job starts at 20.267 s, when
   the first `.info` arrives, and its batches are assembled by 20.29 s. A device whose
   `.info` arrives before that is told to come back 1 s later (`first_batch_delay_s: 1`):
   every 200 ms top-up is 2,023 ms. On the 128 kbit/s line the queued `.info` requests
   arrive after 20.29 s, find the batch held and download at once (mean 1,690, max
   2,199 ms). The 5 ms line also polls more (272 against 264 messages), which fails the
   test's last assertion. The pre-generated batch the test describes (RAPRE: "ready when
   the `.info` answer arrives") is not what the RA holds: with `max_periods_ahead` 156 the
   period is due at acceptance, so in the deployment it was generated long before the
   device asks. The lazy job is a cost deferral that leaks a fictitious generation wait.
3. **`::the_pseudonym_strategy_moves_linkability_and_p2pcd`** (897 against 731 unknown
   signers). All 20 vehicles spawn at once, so their BSMs fall in an 11 ms window of each
   100 ms and hidden-terminal collisions are common. The first frame after a silence does
   carry the new certificate (node 3: change 5.056 s, certificate 8.467 s; node 18: 7.162 s).
   But node 18's was lost at every receiver to node 19's frame 375 us later (hidden
   terminal, SINR -1.5 to -17 dB), and its receivers then lost 9 digest frames each until
   the next certificate. Post-change losses are 353 in the 5 s run and 520 with the
   silence. The assertion compares two single draws of a collision pattern, not a
   mechanism. On seeds 2 and 3 (same scenario files) the silence lowers unknown signers as
   the test expects: 760 -> 579 and 1,009 -> 702. Pooled over seeds 1 to 3, the 5 s run
   loses 2,500 and the silent run 2,178.

Not fixed this session. Weakening any of them is out, and the realistic fix is in the RA:
a period whose due time is well past should be served as held (the weekly job run at the
boundary or at acceptance, with a download request that arrives before the lazy batch is
assembled waiting at the RA, not answered empty). That moves every SCMS digest, so it
needs a re-bless and its own tests. Tests 1 and 2 need that change. Test 3 can keep its inequality over
seeds 1 to 3 pooled (measured: 2,178 <= 2,500). It could also add a record-level check
that the first frame after each silence carries a certificate, which it does here.
Neither change was made: no build was possible to prove the new form red and green.

1. **R-KEMFILE / R-PQUNSEC** (finding 2): `Phase2::real_mbr` now runs the roadside and

   only what the US profile files. A unit's own detection and an unmapped one are refused

2. **P-384 priced as P-256** (finding 3): `ecdsa-p384` signs and verifies with
   `ecdsa-p384-*`. `HardwareProfile::priced_op` charges a profile's own P-384 figure and
   falls back to P-256 where the profile has none, flagged on the cost card ("charged as
   P-256: this profile publishes no P-384 figure"). Server, verify-priced check and the
   HSM lower edge use the charged op. Test `v2xw-node/tests/curve_pricing.rs` (3 tests pass;
   red without the fix: `signature_ops("ecdsa-p384")` returned the P-256 ops).
3. **Detection judged unverified messages** (finding 5): a message the policy delivered

   the `938fb16a` VOD run checked 49,189 messages, 46,203 of them `Unverified`.

## Findings

### Engine defects and deviations (open)

1. **R-P2PCD-ASYM: under `chain_file: home-provider`, provider-b receivers almost never
   learn provider-a's chain** (run CHP; no row predicted it). The CHP rows themselves
   pass: P2PCD answers go on the air (0 -> 77) and the unknown-signer loss rises.
   - `verified_by_provider` (receiver <- signer): provider-b <- provider-a drops from
     82,120 in L to **33**. The other direction, provider-a <- provider-b, only drops from
     82,463 to 82,025 (-0.5 %).
   - IEEE 1609.2 clause 8 P2PCD should let either side learn the other's ACA and ICA
     within a few BSMs.
   - Overall delivery falls from 0.160 to 0.122, and the unknown-signer loss rises from
     0.0075 to 0.046. The run cost 100 s of CPU, against 56 to 76 s for L.
   - `Phase2::provider_chain_files` (phase2.rs:3725) builds both providers' chain files
     the same way, so the asymmetry is in the learning. Two candidates: provider-a's (the
     home PKI's) ACA chains through a certificate the P2PCD answer does not carry; or
     provider-b's receivers learn the ACA but never ask for the ICA above it
     (`NodeSecurity::p2pcd_preprocess`, v2xw-node secure.rs:1128).
   - Leading hypothesis, from the code: `NodeSecurity::p2pcd_learn` (secure.rs:1206)
     drops a learned CA whose own issuer is unknown (`learn_unanchored`). A P2PCD request
     is only raised for a signer's issuer (`on_unknown_issuer`, secure.rs:1168), never
     for the dropped ACA's ICA. So a receiver that lacks the other provider's ICA can never
     anchor that provider's ACA. 1609.2 clause 8 has the requester ask next for the issuer
     of the certificate it could not validate. The asymmetry would follow if only one
     provider's ACA chains through an ICA the other provider's file lacks.
   - Next step: re-run CHP at `--record-level receptions` and count the `node.p2pcd`
     `learn_unanchored` and learned Id3s by the receiver's provider.
2. **R-KEMFILE: a post-quantum KEM changes which misbehaviour reports are filed** (run
   KEM; no row).
   - The chain: `security.kem: ml-kem-768` turns the 1609.2.1 PDU layer off (`ScmsRun`,
     run/mod.rs:1174). `Phase2::real_mbr` then returns `Off` before the US profile's
     gates (phase2.rs:5044), and `files_report` files every `Off` report (phase2.rs:4851).
   - Measured: `reports_not_filed` 255 -> 0, `reports_received` 327 -> 582 (+78 %), the
     uplink +66 %.
   - The SM-MBR filing rules do not depend on the encryption scheme: a subtype must map
     the detector's checks, and there is no roadside reporter. So the 255 detections the
     US profile refuses should stay on the device under a PQ KEM too.
   - This is the vehicle side of R-PQUNSEC (06 §6 item 10, an owner decision). The fix is
     the same reorder: run the subtype and roadside gates before the PDU-layer check.
   - Revocations do not change here (2 -> 2): the extra reports are about the same
     attackers.
3. **P-384 is priced as P-256** (row S384.verify, open).
   - `profile::signature_ops` keeps the P-256 operations for every classical curve. So
     `ecdsa-p384` costs exactly what P-256 does: verify 0.422 ms, sign 9.012 ms, and the
     rate is still keyed `ecdsa-p256-verify`.
   - This holds even where a P-384 figure exists: `obu-generic-automotive-soc-no-hsm`
     publishes 8,104 us sign and 5,721 us verify (R5 B.5).
   - The on-air size is right: +37 B against the predicted +36.8 B (+32 B of signature,
     plus 48 B on each certificate × 0.1).
   - Proposed fix: use the scheme's own operation where the profile prices it, and P-256
     otherwise, with the fallback flagged.
4. **The KEM download is smaller than per-certificate KEM theory** (row KEM.dl-bytes
   passes on direction only).
   - Under ML-KEM-768, `ScmsSizes::batch_download` charges certificates × (pseudonym +
