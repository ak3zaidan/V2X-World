**The Falcon AoI check.** In both Falcon runs `delivery_ratio` is 0, while 20,826 frames
decoded at the PHY. The cause is `net_mtu_refusals` = 80 (40 vehicles × 2 s × 1 Hz). A
Falcon-512 SPDU that carries its certificate (an 897-octet key and 666-octet signatures)
does not fit the WSMP MTU, so every certificate frame is refused. No receiver ever learns a
signer, nothing is delivered, and AoI is undefined. This is correct behaviour: a real
stack also needs fragmentation for a post-quantum certificate. The check now sets
`net.fragmenter: generic-sdu` in its `base_set`, and the PRR and CBR checks keep their
setup. Re-run result: see §4.

**Floor** is each run's own PRR at 40 to 200 m (0.978 to 1.0). It holds the collision
share, which the laws leave out.

**Mutation.** Running `radioval.py check --mutate` turns every row red (0 of 24 green).
The first mutation pass left four rows green: both σ = 6 rows (a d0 × 0.85 fault stayed
inside the 0.08 tolerance), the step row, and the CBR bound. Their faults are now the
defects those rows exist to catch: shadowing not applied, the sensitivity cut missing,
and overlapping busy time counted twice. The expectations themselves did not change.

**Run cost** (`/usr/bin/time`, release binary): the 4 s, 40-vehicle runs take 1.0 to
1.3 s and 33 to 36 MB. At 10 Hz for 3 s, 40 vehicles take 2.0 s and 42 MB, 80 take 6.0 s
and 73 MB, and 160 take 20.4 s and 152 MB. Doubling the vehicles costs ×3.0 then ×3.4,
close to the N² × rate the medium tier predicts (every frame is evaluated at every
receiver in range). Memory is linear (×1.7, ×2.1).

**Agreement.** The fading rows sit 0.003 to 0.044 below theory. That is consistent with the
PER waterfall: decoding is not a hard step at −92 dBm, so the effective edge is about
2 % inside d0 (the clean edge measured 760 m against 766 m). Every miss has the same
sign, which reads as a small bias and not as noise.

## 5. Open items

- **Sidelink candidate set (X %).** At 40 vehicles almost every resource is free. A larger
  X spreads picks onto resources that far UEs already use (under the RSRP exclusion
  threshold), so more collisions at low load is physically possible. The row's "down"
  holds where the resource pool is contended. Settling it needs a dense LTE base, with the
  row moved there if confirmed. Not changed without that evidence.
- **Sidelink selection window.** A smaller T2 should lower the mean SPS latency
  (TS 38.214 8.1.4). The measured +6 % may be an engine defect: the resource reselected
  outside [n + T1, n + T2], or the window not narrowing the candidates. Needs a record
  check of `grant` offsets against T2.
- **Sidelink NF row.** PRR at 600 to 1,000 m is 0.96 on the LTE grid, so the edge is past
  1 km and 3 dB of SNR moves the band by 0.02 %. The row needs a band at the sidelink
  edge.
- **J2945/1 power response (SUPRA)** was not reached: CBR peaked at 0.31 against the
  50 % vMinChanUtil. A row needs a base above 0.5 CBR.
- **LTE-V2X mode-4 SPS collisions against TR 37.885 curves:** no digitised TR 37.885
  curve was available. The relative density and sensing rows in
  `test-mac-congestion.md` stand.
- **PDR against published 802.11p field tests (Bai 2010, CAMP VSC-A):** no digitised curve
  was available. The rows test each law's own closed form instead.
- **Cellular backhaul** latency per preset was not covered (`net.uu` presets are measured
  one-way figures).
- `docs/validation/network.md` (generated) still lists NET-03 as FAIL. It needs
  `cargo run -p v2xw-netval --bin netval-report`, which includes the slow end-to-end
  runs.
